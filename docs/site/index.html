Monte Carlo — inside outram-mc-libs

A Rust port of selected OpenMC kernels: CSG geometry and nested lattices, surface and delta tracking, collisions, k-eigenvalue power iteration, tallies, variance reduction and multigroup mode. Each lesson sits beside the code it describes.

Tutorial: Monte Carlo, one rung at a time

Lessons that start from a question and answer it with the real code, one rung of physics at a time, each with its rung of the live demo. Rung 2, uranium in graphite: slowing down, S(α,β), resonance escape, the four-factor formula, and why a homogeneous natural-uranium mixture fails. Rung 3, lumping: spatial self-shielding, and k∞ against lump size. Rung 4, LEU-COMP-THERM-008: water, rods and lattices.

Live demo: the Monte Carlo demo, a rung at a time

One app with a rung setting. Godiva, a bare sphere of uranium: watch real neutron histories, then run a true k-eigenvalue calculation in your browser, one console line per generation. Uranium in graphite and a uranium lump in graphite: watch neutrons slow down through a hundred-odd carbon collisions, and see where U-238's resonances catch them. LCT-008: one neutron at a time through a real benchmark lattice of 4961 rods. TRISO pebble: one neutron at a time through a 2D analogue of an HTR-10 fuel pebble. Every neutron is transported by outram-mc-libs on real ENDF/B-VIII.0 data, which your browser downloads and processes with the workspace's NJOY port in a background worker before the first neutron flies — expect a minute or two (more for Godiva's Run k_eff, which processes at NJOY's full tolerance); the page stays responsive meanwhile, and on a phone the control panel folds away to the left.

Nuclear data — inside njoy-outram-park-fork

Where a Monte Carlo code's cross sections come from, one NJOY module at a time: what an ENDF tape holds, RECONR building σ(E) from resonance parameters (and the Fe-57 bug case study), BROADR and temperature, the unresolved range and probability tables, thermal scattering, heating and gas, multigroup constants, covariances, and the ACE table a transport code reads. Every module of the port has a row in the coverage table, including the ones not ported.
