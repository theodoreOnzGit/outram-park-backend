OUTRAM PARK — code deep dives

How the outram-park-backend crates are built, and why, read beside their code and API reference. The concept lessons for students will live on the OUTRAM PARK teaching site (not live yet). The code is translated from many open-source projects: see Credits & upstream code.

This site always tracks the develop branch, never main. It is rebuilt on every push to develop, and every code link points at the exact develop commit it was built from (see the footer). A released version on main may differ from what you read here.

Research, education and V&V only. Not for reactor operation, licensing, safety decisions or emergency response. These pages are AI-assisted drafts until a human has reviewed them; each page states its review status.

Monte Carlo — inside outram-mc-libs

A Rust port of selected OpenMC kernels: CSG geometry and nested lattices, surface and delta tracking, collisions, k-eigenvalue power iteration, tallies, variance reduction and multigroup mode. Each lesson sits beside the code it describes.

Tutorial: Monte Carlo, one rung at a time

Lessons that start from a question and answer it with the real code, one rung of physics at a time, each with its rung of the live demo. Rung 1, Godiva: free flights, the surface, collisions, fission generations, leakage and fast fission by counting, and whether a sphere of uranium is critical. Rung 2, uranium in graphite: slowing down, S(α,β), resonance escape, the four-factor formula, and why a homogeneous natural-uranium mixture fails. Rung 3, lumping: spatial self-shielding, and k∞ against lump size. Rung 4, LEU-COMP-THERM-008: water, rods and lattices. Rung 5, TRISO: lumps inside lumps, delta tracking and its majorant, double-heterogeneity shortcuts, ending in the HTR-10 pebble bed.

Live demo: the Monte Carlo demo, a rung at a time

One app with a rung setting. Godiva, a bare sphere of uranium: watch real neutron histories, then run a true k-eigenvalue calculation in your browser, one console line per generation. Uranium in graphite and a uranium lump in graphite: watch neutrons slow down through a hundred-odd carbon collisions, and see where U-238's resonances catch them. LCT-008: one neutron at a time through a real benchmark lattice of 4961 rods, with a pitch slider that runs a small pin-cell k∞ in your browser. HTR-10: zoom from the core to a pebble, a TRISO particle and its kernel; the recorded k against loading height; and a fuel-zone k∞ with the kernels resolved and smeared, run in your browser (a few minutes). TRISO pebble: one neutron at a time through a 2D analogue of an HTR-10 fuel pebble. Every neutron is transported by outram-mc-libs on real ENDF/B-VIII.0 data, which your browser downloads and processes with the workspace's NJOY port in a background worker before the first neutron flies — expect a minute or two (more for Godiva's Run k_eff, which processes at NJOY's full tolerance); the page stays responsive meanwhile, and on a phone the control panel folds away to the left.

Nuclear data — inside njoy-outram-park-fork

Where a Monte Carlo code's cross sections come from, one NJOY module at a time: what an ENDF tape holds, RECONR building σ(E) from resonance parameters (and the Fe-57 bug case study), BROADR and temperature, the unresolved range and probability tables, thermal scattering, heating and gas, multigroup constants, covariances, and the ACE table a transport code reads. Every module of the port has a row in the coverage table, including the ones not ported.

Live demo: nuclear data, a rung at a time

Real ENDF/B-VIII.0 evaluations processed in your browser by the workspace's NJOY port, in a background worker: RECONR building U-238's cross section as you watch, BROADR's temperature slider, PURR's probability bands, thermal scattering for graphite and water, and a group collapse with self-shielding. About 11 MB of data for all rungs (3 MB for U-238 alone); the page stays responsive throughout.

Site and demo tooling

The site is built with these tools; no code from them is ported, and the credit for them belongs to their maintainers.

  • egui, eframe and egui_plot (Emil Ernerfeldt and contributors; MIT OR Apache-2.0): every live demo's user interface.
  • wasm-bindgen, js-sys and web-sys (the wasm-bindgen developers; MIT OR Apache-2.0): runs the demos in your browser.
  • mdBook (the Rust project; MPL-2.0): the lessons and deep dives.
  • rustdoc (the Rust project; MIT OR Apache-2.0): the API reference pages.
  • rust-analyzer (the Rust project; MIT OR Apache-2.0): checks the code walks in the lessons.
  • Elsewhere in the workspace, the kovan GUI translates part of James Taylor's markdown-editor (ISC) and uses the gruvbox colour values (MIT).

Methods and data

The Monte Carlo kernels are a Rust port of selected OpenMC routines (Romano et al., 2015). Cross sections are processed by a port of NJOY (MacFarlane et al., 2017) from the ENDF/B-VIII.0 evaluations (Brown & others, 2018), and the criticality benchmarks are cases of the ICSBEP handbook (Briggs et al., 2003). The suite itself is described in (Ong, 2026), and its verification and validation in the two arXiv papers (Ong et al., 2026) (Ong & Xiao, 2026).
