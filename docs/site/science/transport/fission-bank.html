The fission bank and k

Transport

Until now the neutron was a stranger who appeared from nowhere. In a criticality problem there is no outside source: the neutrons of one generation are born at the fissions of the previous one. That makes k the ratio

k = (neutrons in one generation) / (neutrons in the generation before)

and Monte Carlo finds it by power iteration: run a generation, collect its fission sites, make them the next generation's source, repeat.

Banking the sites

When a collision turns out to be a fission (page 5) the neutron's history ends, and the code banks the new neutrons: their position (the fission site), an isotropic direction, an energy from the nuclide's own fission spectrum, and, for delayed neutrons, a precursor group with its own softer spectrum. How many? The expected number is ν̄/k, with ν̄ the neutrons per fission and k the running eigenvalue. Dividing by k keeps the bank near the same size from generation to generation. The integer is a floor plus a Bernoulli draw on the remainder (sample_num_neutrons): ⌊ν̄/k⌋ + [ξ < frac(ν̄/k)].

One generation, one number

The estimate of k from a generation is its fission production per source neutron, kgen = ∑(w ν̄)/n, summed over every fission in the generation (production / n_particles, run_keff_csg_seq_progress). The reported k is the mean of the active generations' kgen; the first few (inactive) generations are thrown away, because the first source is a guess: a box of points rejected into the fissionable cells.

Back to a fixed size

The bank after a generation is not exactly n sites. It must be brought back to n. Until 2026-09-30 the code drew n sites with replacement; it now uses OpenMC's uniform comb (comb_resample): n evenly spaced teeth with one random offset, so every site is taken either ⌊n/total⌋ or ⌈n/total⌉ times. Sampling with replacement is unbiased for one generation but adds multinomial noise, and power iteration with a finite bank already carries a small negative bias of order 1/N (gh:#460).

Predict first: resample 10 banked sites down to 8. With the comb, how many sites can end up dropped entirely? With replacement?

Banking and combing

Ports of sample_num_neutrons and comb_resample (outram-mc-libs/src/physics/fission.rs), JavaScript sketch with a handful of sites so the multiplicities are legible; the real banks hold thousands. check-transport.js verifies that the mean sites per fission is ν̄/k, that the comb returns exactly n sites each taken ⌊n/total⌋ or ⌈n/total⌉ times, and that sampling with replacement breaks that.

Has the source settled? Shannon entropy

k alone is a poor test of convergence: it is an integral and can look settled long before the shape of the fission source has. Given a spatial mesh, the code computes the Shannon entropy of the fission source in each generation, from the bank before resampling (ported from OpenMC): when it stops drifting the shape has settled. Using it to judge the inactive generations goes back to Brown's study of the fission-source convergence of criticality calculations (F. B. Brown, 2006).
