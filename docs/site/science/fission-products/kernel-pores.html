Voids climb the temperature gradient

Fission products

Fuel makes its heat inside and loses it at the surface, so its centre is hotter than its edge. In fuel where that difference is large, the pores themselves move.

Predict first. A pore is a little empty bubble. Does it drift towards the hot centre, towards the cool edge, or stay put?

Up the gradient

A pore drifts towards the hot side. The usual explanation is that material evaporates from the pore’s hot face, crosses it, and condenses on its cold face, so the pore moves the other way. The pores leave long grains behind them pointing at the centre (columnar grains), and gather there as a central hole. This is seen in high-power fuel with steep gradients, for example in fast reactors. source pending for each of these statements.

Why the kernel's gradient is so small

For a sphere with uniform heating, the centre-to-surface rise is the power density times the radius squared over six times the conductivity. The rise goes as the radius squared: a kernel a few hundred micrometres across has almost none, which is one reason small fuel stays cool inside.
