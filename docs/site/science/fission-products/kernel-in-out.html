In the grain, and out of it

Fission products

A fission product is born inside a grain. To leave the kernel it must first reach a grain boundary, and then get from there to the kernel’s surface. Each family behaves differently at each stage. The row of the family you chose is in bold.

Predict first. Once a xenon atom reaches a grain boundary, is it out?

Gases: not at once

A gas atom diffuses through the grain, now and then caught by a small bubble and knocked back out by a passing fission fragment (lesson 9). At the boundary it does not leave straight away: it collects in boundary bubbles, which grow and spread. Only when they link up into paths to the outside does the gas vent. SCIANTIX models these steps (Pizzocri et al., 2018): gas_diffusion.rst, intergranular_bubble_behavior.rst and grain_boundary_venting.rst, where venting is a sigmoid in the bubbles’ coverage of the boundary, or a correlation with open porosity. So: at the boundary, not yet out.

Release by bookkeeping

SCIANTIX does not track released gas directly: it is what is left when the gas still in the grains, at the boundaries and lost to decay is taken from what was produced (gas_release.rst).
