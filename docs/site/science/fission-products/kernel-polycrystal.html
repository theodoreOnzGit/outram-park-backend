Grains, boundaries and defects

  • Grains. Many small crystals, each a region of one continuous lattice, with its rows pointing its own way (U.S. Department of Energy, 1993), p. 9.
  • Grain boundaries. Where grains meet, the atoms misfit: a region usually one to three atom diameters wide (U.S. Department of Energy, 1993), p. 9. Disordered, so atoms move along it more easily.
  • Point defects. Missing atoms (vacancies), atoms in the gaps (interstitials), foreign atoms on lattice sites (U.S. Department of Energy, 1993), p. 18. Every fission product is one of those foreign atoms.
  • Not exactly UO2. The oxygen content can drift: UO2+x. SCIANTIX models this deviation and the oxygen potential that comes with it (Pizzocri et al., 2018), stoichiometry_deviation.rst.

Some TRISO kernels are UCO (a mix of oxide and carbide) instead. This chapter is about UO2 only; UCO is mentioned, not modelled.

Why this matters for release

A fission product released from the kernel has to cross two scales: from inside a grain to its boundary, and from the boundary network to the kernel’s surface. Lessons 16 to 18 model the first as diffusion out of a sphere, and ask what to do about the second.
