A fragment's flight

Fission products

A fission splits the nucleus into two fragments that fly apart back to back (lesson 1). Each is an unstable nucleus and will decay, but decay is slow compared with what happens first, so on this page we ignore it. The question here: where do the fragments go, and what do they do to the fuel on the way?

Slowing down happens in two ways

The fragment is a heavy, highly charged ion pushing through a crowd of electrons and nuclei. It loses energy two ways, and which one wins depends on how fast it is.

  • Fast: electronic stopping. While it is fast, it mostly tears electrons off the atoms it passes (ionisation). That energy ends up as heat. The path is nearly straight, because electrons are too light to deflect it.
  • Slow: nuclear stopping. As it slows, it picks up electrons and its charge drops, and collisions with whole nuclei take over. Now it bounces, wanders, and knocks atoms out of place (next lesson). Then it stops.

The handbook puts the crossover in words: elastic collisions with nuclei mostly happen below about A keV, where A is the mass number of the moving atom; above it, the moving atom “will lose much of its energy in causing ionization” (U.S. Department of Energy, 1993), p. 34. For Sr-95 that is about …, … of its starting energy: almost all the way, it is electronic stopping.

Where it stops, it is a foreign atom in the lattice: a strontium or xenon atom sitting where uranium and oxygen should be. The rest of this chapter follows it from there.

Why the tracks are labelled illustrative

RustBCA’s high-energy electronic stopping uses the fragment’s bare nuclear charge. A real fragment carries many of its electrons, so its effective charge is lower; this matters at fragment speeds. Until the ranges are compared with independent range data, boon-lay’s rules call them illustrative.

The flight is short in time, too

The range over the birth speed is a lower bound on the flight time: … for the light fragment. It slows down, so the true time is longer; RustBCA’s steps will give it once the tracks run. Either way it is far shorter than any half-life on lesson 6’s chains.
