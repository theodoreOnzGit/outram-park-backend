Complication 9: the kernel swells

Fission products

Two fission products take up more room than the one uranium atom they replace, and the gas among them gathers in bubbles. So the kernel swells source pending. In a TRISO particle it has somewhere to go: the buffer around it is porous carbon, with room for the kernel to grow into and for gas to collect.

The clean curve, for comparison

Swelling does not change Booth’s curve directly. What it changes is the room outside: how much free volume is left in the buffer, and so the gas pressure on the coatings. That is where the next part of the story goes. Link back: the many-spheres model stays as it was; the room around it shrinks.

So the kernel lets its fission products go, some fast, some slowly, by chemistry, by size and by temperature. Next, the buffer layer.
