Air on hot graphite

Fission products

So far the particles sat in helium, which does nothing to them. If a pipe breaks and air gets into a hot core, the graphite around the fuel starts to burn. How fast? And what does the code actually know about it?

Graphite in air: the rate, and what limits it

graphite_air::kinetic_specific_rate and gasification_rate, recorded on a 10 °C grid at six O2 pressures. The 5000 kg and 1 mol/s are boon-lay’s own check values, not a plant’s.

Predict first (we did, before running the code): in air, from what temperature does the oxygen supply, not the chemistry, set the rate? We said about 612 °C, still inside the measured range.

Two limits, and the lower one wins

Burning graphite needs two things: the chemistry at the surface, and oxygen arriving. The rate is whichever is slower. The chemistry speeds up very steeply with temperature, so at reactor temperatures every oxygen molecule that arrives reacts at once. Then the supply sets the rate, and making the graphite hotter changes nothing.

The chemistry part is an Arrhenius law, first order in oxygen:

k = A e−Ea/RT pO2/pO2,ref,   Ea = 191 kJ/mol, ln A = 13.0 (A in 1/s)

with k the fraction of the graphite’s mass lost per second, and pO2,ref air’s 21 % of 1 atm. Then the carbon burned is

ṅC = min( k mgraphite/MC,  ṅO2 )

The numbers are from one row of a review: Contescu et al., ORNL/TM-2022/1839, Table 3 (p. 39), row Contescu (2011), IG-110 graphite in air at 597–694 °C. At 650 °C the code gives …, the same as a hand evaluation to 10−4. That is a check of the copy (verification of the transcription), not of the physics.

With the illustrative 5000 kg and 1 mol/s, the supply binds from … in air (predicted about 612 °C) and from … at 1 kPa of O2 (predicted about 731 °C). At 900 °C the chemistry alone would burn … times what the supply allows (predicted about 580). The heat follows the supply too: at the CO2 enthalpy, 1 mol/s of O2 releases ….

The measured range and the extrapolation

The law was measured at 597–694 °C (the shaded band, dotted line). Everything else is extrapolated (dashed). Above the band, real graphite is limited by oxygen diffusing into its pores and through the gas film at its surface (the review’s regimes 2 and 3), so the extrapolated Arrhenius line over-states the chemistry there. The code does not model those regimes; the supply limit is its guard. It says so: every call returns a Validity flag, InsideMeasuredRange or Extrapolated.

Two more assumptions, labelled in the code. First order in oxygen (the review gives no order for IG-110), which is why every curve in the widget has the same shape, shifted. And the reaction enthalpies are at 298 K.

Where it is in the code

graphite_air::gasification_rate branches: kinetic ≤ supply gives Limit::Kinetic, otherwise Limit::OxygenSupply. It calls kinetic_specific_rate, the Arrhenius rate with its flag. Nothing in boon-lay calls them; the htgr_sim_v1 digital twin’s depressurisation model does. Its oxygen supply is an assumed exchange at the reactor cavity’s ventilation rate (gh:#420): no core air-ingress rate is published for HTR-10.

What is missing

A rate of graphite loss is not yet a release. Between the two is a chain of attacks on a pebble, layer by layer, and almost none of it exists (epic gh:#441):

  • The fuel-free graphite shell recedes: rate only, this law.
  • The fuelled matrix recedes and exposes particles; the outer PyC burns off: not modelled.
  • The SiC oxidises and thins, until it fails under pressure: not wired. Lesson 37’s thinning factor could take it (gh:#443).
  • An exposed kernel is attacked. In steam, a fit exists (next lesson). In air, UO2 oxidising to U3O8, which swells and lets go of what the lattice held, is missing (gh:#444).

So no particle in this workspace fails because of oxidation. Where a source term needs that, sembawang adds measured failures by hand (the last lesson of chapter 5).

Next: steam instead of air: it gasifies the graphite more slowly, and it does something to a bare kernel that air (in this code) does not.

The one contact with measured data this law could have

The only check so far is the transcription. A first comparison with measured data would be IAEA-TECDOC-978’s Fig. 5-18, fuel-compact weight loss in air at 1400 °C (gh:#457). It is not done.

Why first order matters only sometimes

The assumed oxygen order changes the kinetic rate, so it matters only where the kinetics limit: below the crossover. Above it the supply sets the answer whatever the order. Move the O2 slider and watch the crossover move while the right-hand answer above it stays put.
