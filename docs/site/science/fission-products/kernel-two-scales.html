The same D, three radii

Predict first. If boundaries are fast highways out, which radius is right: the grain’s or the kernel’s?

Open paths and closed paths

  • Open paths: the boundaries connect to the outside and carry atoms out quickly. Reaching a boundary is release, so the sphere is the grain.
  • Closed paths: the boundaries hold atoms (lesson 14), and the atoms must cross the whole kernel by slow diffusion. The sphere is the kernel.

Real fuel is somewhere between. TRISO-ATOPS takes a blend: for its “special metals” it uses a sphere of radius a = √(2 agrain r), with r the kernel radius. That is what boon-lay’s port computes (rb_fail, read 2026-10-20), and its derivation document gives the same form (TRISO_ATOPS_DERIVATION.md, §2). What the blend does

The blended radius is the geometric mean of the grain diameter and the kernel radius. Booth depends on D/a², so the blend divides the kernel’s D/r² by 2agrain/r: release is faster than whole-kernel diffusion and slower than grain-only release.

Note that TRISO-ATOPS’s code describes its grain size as the UCO fuel grain size (its rb_fail documentation).
