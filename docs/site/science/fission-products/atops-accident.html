Accidents: Booth on the integral, and venting

Fission products

Chapter 4’s lesson 37 released a failed particle’s contents by Booth along a changing temperature. That was TRISO-ATOPS’s own accident model, borrowed early. Here is the rest of it: the graphite gives up what it held, and the coolant gas carries the release out of the vessel, but only while it is being pushed out.

One node through a heat-up

release_fraction_transient on integrate_diffusion_over_time, and coolant_release. The heat-up is an illustrative prescribed history, not a plant calculation. One node, MHTGR Case A geometry.

Predict first (we did, before running the code): the gas vented by the end of the hold is 1 − T0/Tpeak (ideal gas in a fixed volume), within 2 %; and nothing more is vented once the core cools.

D·t becomes the integral of D

Booth’s series and the silver breakthrough depend on D·t. When the temperature changes, the code replaces D·t by ∫D dt, summed sample by sample (trapezoids). Kernel and graphite each get their own integral; noble gases and halogens are given nothing in the graphite. At the end of the hold of the 1600 °C heat-up, … of the caesium has left the kernels (predicted: at least 99 %).

Two simplifications ride along. The accident uses the kernel radius as Booth’s sphere, not normal operation’s smaller √(2 agrain r) (lesson 42). The paper uses one sphere for both; boon-lay offers that as the option atops2026paper (see “Learn more” below). And the 5000-term floor of lesson 42 is there too, snapped to zero only below 10−6, which the floor is not.

Venting only

The release has to leave the vessel. In the model the only driver is the gas expanding as the core heats after a breach: the fraction of coolant pushed out follows the ideal gas law. When the core starts to cool, the gas contracts and draws air back in, and the release stops (Stoyer et al. 2026, pp. 4 and 13, which call this “breathing”). The black line ends there; the shaded part is release that stays in the core.

By the end of the hold the code has vented … of the coolant, against … for an ideal gas. That missed the prediction: the code takes the temperature’s rate of change as zero at the first sample, so its trapezoid sum drops half of the first 5 h step. The missing piece is half of the first step, so a finer time grid shrinks it. The spike to 1 at time zero is upstream’s too: its first venting sample is set to 1 in the code, whatever the integral says (coolant_release’s docs list it as a preserved quirk).

There is no ingress transport: no air or water flowing through and carrying the release out while the core cools or sits still. An isothermal hold once gave 0 Bq in the port; that was a porting defect, fixed (gh:#446): upstream vents fully when the temperature is uniform and constant. Ingress flow is added outside the port, by sembawang, and labelled not upstream.

Where it is in the code

The port has the accident’s pieces, not one driver (gh:#447): integrate_diffusion_over_time, release_fraction_transient (which calls booth_transient, breakthrough_model_transient or rf_graph), release_activity (what is left to release, after the failed fractions and what normal operation already moved), mean_temperature_rate and coolant_release. sembawang assembles them.

The physical accident

The theory book follows a loss-of-cooling accident step by step and lists what TRISO-ATOPS does not simulate: step 16.

The atops2026paper option

boon-lay’s port runs upstream’s code by default (TrisoAtopsVariant::Upstream). Under the option TrisoAtopsVariant::ATOPS2026PAPER, the paper’s equations as written (gh:#1015), the accident’s Booth sphere is normal operation’s radius, …, not the … kernel radius (pp. 8, 11–12). The smaller sphere releases more: the kernel release rises by the ratio of the radii, 1.41. The option also gives palladium silver’s laws, and scales the plate-out in the accident inventory by irradiation time over run time (pp. 11–13). In code, the variant is an argument of the accident functions ending in _for, such as release_fraction_transient_for.

The paper’s printed release numbers come from upstream’s code, not from its written equations. Run as written, the model misses most of the paper’s printed accident releases. Run as the code, it reproduces every initial release (with the plate-out constant gh:#413 found misprinted). The tutorial case Reproducing the TRISO-ATOPS 2026 paper’s release results shows how this was found, with the source code of the test.

Other pieces of the accident

The circulating coolant is released at once when the boundary breaks, and a user-chosen fraction of the plated-out activity lifts off (pp. 4 and 12). Only nuclides with a half-life longer than 4 % of the accident are followed (p. 11).
