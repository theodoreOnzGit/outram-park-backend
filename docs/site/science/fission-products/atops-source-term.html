The chain

boon-lay (failure, release) → sembawang (source term) → changi (dispersion)

  • sembawang orchestrates. It takes a core inventory, the failure fractions in TRISO-ATOPS’s classes, and a temperature history, runs the port’s release pieces (lessons 39 and 51) for every nuclide and every node, and assembles the result. None of the release physics is in sembawang; every piece is a call into boon-lay’s triso_atops_fork.
  • changi carries the release downwind: plume, puff, deposition. Its own track is the dispersion deep dive.

What crosses each seam

boon-lay to sembawang:

  • The failed fractions from an accident history, φ1 and φ2 (FailureProgress), into TRISO-ATOPS’s full-failure class through with_fuel_failure_incremental (lesson 49).
  • Per-nuclide release fractions and activities, as uom quantities.
  • The 84-nuclide table: which nuclides the release can carry at all (lesson 46). A nuclide not in it is an error, not a silent zero (gh:#449).

sembawang to changi: a SourceTerm: contiguous release windows, and for each nuclide the activity released during each window. TRISO-ATOPS gives cumulative releases; sembawang takes first differences, so the windows add up to the last cumulative value exactly. A window that would come out negative is reported (negative_atom_count_seen), then floored to zero at the boundary, because SourceTerm rejects negative activity. In this record that caveat is on, for silver and Cs-137.

What does not cross: anything from the random walk of chapter 2 and lesson 34. It is verified against exact solutions, but no source-term path uses it.

The recorded release

The widget shows one recorded run: the whole HTR-10 core held at 1400 °C, every particle exposed, the full-failure fraction raised by the measured KORA failures (an irradiated sphere heated in air, IAEA-TECDOC-978 Table 5-7) added by hand, because no model in the workspace makes a particle fail by oxidation (previous lessons). Over 96 h it releases … in all; the six nuclides drawn carry … of it (predicted 65 %).

The fractions answer the predict prompt. Xe-133 and I-131 leave in the same fraction, … and …: both are released through failed particles only, and neither is held in the graphite. Cs-137 leaves in …, and silver in the largest, …, because it diffuses through intact SiC (lesson 33) and so does not need a failed particle at all.

The gaps that travel downstream

A source term inherits every gap above it. From these chapters:

  • Transport out of the core (gh:#446, closed). TRISO-ATOPS moves release out only by heat-up venting (lesson 51). An isothermal air-ingress hold once released 0 Bq in the port; reading upstream showed that was a porting defect, now fixed: upstream vents fully when the temperature is uniform and constant, which is what this record ran. A real ingress flow is not upstream physics at all: sembawang adds it (Venting::Ventilation, FullFlowThrough, Prescribed), each labelled not upstream, and a non-uniform hold held constant is now an error rather than a silent zero (gh:#447). With HTR-10’s cavity ventilation instead of full venting this release is 0.950 of what is drawn.
  • No oxidation failure, no air attack on a kernel (gh:#441, #444): the KORA failures stand in, by hand.
  • The free-heavy-metal fraction (gh:#383). German fuel-qualification data give a band of 7.8–50.7×10−6, stored and unchecked. Noble-gas and halogen release is linear in it, so the band alone is a factor of 6.5 on those bars.
  • What boon-lay fuel failure can say for HTR-10 (gh:#296): almost nothing at normal operating temperature, and its inputs are stand-ins. In this record its own increment is 3.8×10−7 beside the 1.2×10−3 of KORA.
  • Atoms that leave the books (gh:#588). The TRISO-ATOPS demo recomputes the graphite hold-up at each step, so on heating the metals held in the graphite vanish instead of being released. Upstream’s accident path, which sembawang uses, releases them; the demo does not yet.
  • The Booth floor (gh:#385, lesson 48): over-states some metal releases at low temperature.

Where it is in the code

sembawang::accident::release::accident_release_with_venting is the driver: the node loops, the venting choice and the windows. bridge_node is the one place normal operation’s six pools become the accident’s seven fields (the seventh is the node’s inventory in atoms). The result, an AccidentRelease, carries the SourceTerm for changi and the caveats, the nuclides screened out by half-life, and the unfloored cumulative release to compare with upstream.

What the record leaves out

The half-life screen drops nuclides with a half-life below 4 % of the transient: here Kr-83m, Kr-87, Kr-88, I-132 and I-134. H-3, Xe-135m and Rb-88 are not in TRISO-ATOPS’s table. The failure fractions stay at their 140 h values over the 96 h window. All of this is in the example’s own doc comment.

Not a comparison

Liu & Cao (2002) give doses for different HTR-10 accidents with different codes and measured weather. They are context, not a check of this number, and this page compares nothing against them.
