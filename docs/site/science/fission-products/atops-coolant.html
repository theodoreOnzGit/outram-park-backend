Into the coolant

Fission products

Once out of the fuel and the graphite, a fission product is in the helium. It can stay there, stick to a cool wall (plate out), be taken out by the purification system, or decay. TRISO-ATOPS keeps track of these as pools with simple first-order rates.

Three pools and their zeros

The circulating pool is fed by the source and drained by decay, plate-out and clean-up; the plate-out and clean-up pools fill from it. Each is a one-line balance with a closed form. The model sets several terms to zero, and the picture draws them:

  • Noble gases do not plate out, being inert (p. 11).
  • The clean-up system takes only gases and halogens. Caesium and strontium plate out before it can reach them (p. 11). So the answer to the predict prompt is no.
  • Gases and halogens are not held in the graphite (p. 9).

The theory book derives the balances and their numbers for HTR-10 (step 13); they are not repeated here.

Not a port: why boon-lay adds to it

A port must agree with upstream, simplifications and all. Where boon-lay needs more, it puts it beside the port, labelled not a port, and tests that it reduces to the port when its addition is switched off:

  • live_pools::step steps the pools from any state, not only from empty, and adds a leak, so a transient can carry them forward. Stepped from empty with no leak over 40 years it reproduces the port’s circulating pool exactly for all five nuclides here (predicted: to 10−9 for Cs-137).
  • triso_atops_extensions gives plate-out and clean-up rates per element instead of one per group. It exists because a picture of all 19 HTR-10 nuclides at once showed iodine, caesium, strontium and silver plating out at one rate, and no metal ever being purified, where HTR-10’s own data differ (gh:#583).

Where it is in the code

normal_operation_node routes the rates by group and calls the closed forms in coolant_activity (circulating, plate_out, clean_up).

The plate-out constant

The paper bases its plate-out rate on 1 % of the inventory per coolant cycle in a large gas-cooled reactor (p. 11), and the clean-up rate on a purification system taking 35 % of the coolant per hour at 90 % efficiency, after Fort St. Vrain’s (p. 11). boon-lay’s verification against the paper’s printed pools found the printed plate-out constant matches only if it is ten times larger; the port keeps it as printed (theory book step 15).
