Settled: decay or move?

Fission products

A stopped fission product has two things it can do. It can decay (lesson 6), or it can move: atoms in a hot solid wander, slowly, from site to site. That is diffusion. Which wins is a race: its half-life against how far it gets in that time.

The race, in a TRISO kernel

D: TRISO-ATOPS’s NP-MHTGR correlations as boon-lay ports them (diffusion_coefficient, kernel). Half-lives: boon-lay’s decay library. Kernel radius …: HTR-10’s, from outram-mc-libs’ TrisoSpec::HTR10_LI2014.

Predict first. Heat the kernel by a few hundred degrees. Does the distance an atom covers in one half-life grow a little, or a lot?

The race

In one half-life t½, an atom diffusing with coefficient D spreads over a distance of about √(6 D t½). If that is much smaller than the kernel, the atom decays where it stopped. If it is comparable or larger, it can reach the edge and get out. Move the temperature slider: the bars jump by orders of magnitude, because D does.

And remember lesson 6: each decay sets off a few more cascades, wherever the atom happens to be when it decays.

The Arrhenius law

How fast an atom diffuses rises steeply with temperature. Every diffusion correlation in the demo has the form of the Arrhenius law, the law chemists use for how reaction rates rise with temperature (Flowers et al., 2019), §12.5, p. 612:

D = D0 exp(−Q / RT)

A barrier Q and the temperature T set an exponential. That is why a few hundred degrees change D by orders of magnitude. The correlations in the demo are exactly this form, some with two terms.

Where √(6Dt) comes from

For a random walk in three dimensions, the mean squared distance from the start grows as 6Dt. Lesson 9 builds this from single hops.

What the correlations are, and where they stop

The NP-MHTGR correlations in TRISO-ATOPS are stated for roughly 700–2400 °C. Below their limits some are clamped (held at their value there): in the kernel, Cs and Sr below 700 °C, read from boon-lay’s code and found again by the lesson generator from the function’s own output. Kr, Xe, I, Te and Se are extrapolated instead. Kr and Xe share one correlation, which switches form at 1500 °C in this code; another boon-lay path switches at 700 °C for Kr, and which is right is open (gh:#974), so neither is presented as settled here.

Booth and release

Turning a diffusion coefficient into the fraction of atoms that leave a grain or a kernel is the Booth model, worked through in boon-lay’s book Fission Product Transport Theory.
