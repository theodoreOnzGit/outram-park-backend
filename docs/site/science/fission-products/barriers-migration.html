Migration through the layers

Fission products

An atom that leaves the kernel still has four layers to cross. In each one it does the same random walk as in the kernel (lessons 8 and 9), but at a different speed. One number sets that speed: the diffusion coefficient D of that element in that material, at that temperature.

D in every layer, and the time to cross it

Solid: boon-lay’s random-walk coefficients, try_get_diffusion_coeff_jiang (Jiang et al. 2023). Dashed: TRISO-ATOPS’s diffusion_coefficient (graphite) and diffusion_coefficient_sic_ag. Thicknesses: HTR-10.

Predict first (we did, before running the code): for Cs, Sr and Ag, crossing the SiC takes at least ten times longer than crossing the inner PyC, at every temperature from 800 to 1600 °C. For krypton, even the inner PyC takes more than a year at 1000 °C.

One law, many numbers

Hops need a borrowed energy from the lattice, so D grows steeply with temperature: the Arrhenius law of lesson 8, D(T) = D1 e−Q1/RT + D2 e−Q2/RT, with constants for each element in each layer. The time to cross a layer of thickness l is about l²/D: double the layer, four times the time.

At 1200 °C, caesium needs … to cross the inner PyC and … to cross the SiC. Krypton needs … to cross the inner PyC at 1000 °C. Both predictions held; the run is in the generator’s docs and check-fission.js.

Two things stand out. The buffer is not a barrier at all: it is porous, so atoms rush through its voids (D = 10−8 m²/s). And the layers differ by many orders of magnitude, element by element. That is the question of lesson 31.

The gaps, shown as gaps

  • Only four elements have their own numbers: Ag, Cs, Sr and Kr. Every other element is walked as if it were silver. That is a placeholder, not a model (gh:#541).
  • Krypton in the buffer has no number. The code there is a todo!(): asking for it would crash. This page never asks; the widget shows a “?”.
  • The graphite around the particles has no number in the walker (also todo!()). TRISO-ATOPS has its own graphite correlation, drawn dashed.
  • Kr’s kernel coefficient switches branch at 700 °C in the walker but at 1500 °C in TRISO-ATOPS (gh:#974, still open).
