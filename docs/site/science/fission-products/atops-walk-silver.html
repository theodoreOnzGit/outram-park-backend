Walk: why silver is different

Fission products · concept walk 3 of 3

Every other group in TRISO-ATOPS leaves only from failed particles. Silver is the exception: it diffuses through intact SiC. The model for that is a membrane with a time lag, and the lag is why silver can be exactly zero in one run and dominant in a hotter one.

  1. Its own group

    Ag (and Pd with it) is the … group. Ag-110m has a half-life of …, long-lived against a few days, short against the 40-year run.

  2. Its own diffusion law, in SiC

    Silver is the only species the code gives a law for intact SiC: … at 900 °C, … at 1600 °C. Its 215 kJ/mol activation energy makes it climb steeply with temperature.

  3. Breakthrough, with a time lag

    The SiC layer is treated as a membrane: nothing comes through until the diffusion front crosses it, then steady permeation. Over the MHTGR irradiation the code’s breakthrough fraction is … at 750 °C, … at 800 °C, … at 850 °C, … at 900 °C and … at 1000 °C. Below the lag the series and the lag term cancel to zero.

  4. From every particle

    For volatiles and metals the release rate is weighted by the failed fractions. For silver the weight is 1: the breakthrough model already speaks for intact particles. So a 1 Ci node releases … atoms/s at 750 °C, … at 800 °C and … at 900 °C: from exactly nothing to the same order as caesium’s … (from failed particles only) within 150 °C.

  5. Zero at normal operation in the demo

    The demo runs HTR-10’s inventory at 776 °C. There the breakthrough has not started, and every silver pool at the end of normal operation is exactly zero: source … atoms/s, circulating … Bq, plated … Bq. A zero like this is a modelling result, not a measurement: it depends on the time lag and the irradiation temperature.

  6. Compare caesium at the same moment

    Caesium, held by intact SiC and released only from failed particles, is not zero there: … Bq circulate. Silver is the group that switches from nothing to something with temperature alone.

  7. Hot: the lag is crossed

    Heat the core and the lag is crossed in hours, not years. In the demo’s 1800 °C hold, by … Ag-110m enters the helium at … atoms/s and … Bq has plated out. (The demo’s hold uses normal operation’s steady model at the hold temperature, not the accident model.) In the lesson’s accident model, on the integral of D, the 1600 °C heat-up releases … of the silver by the end of its hold.

  8. Then it is a metal again

    Once in the helium, silver is routed like the other metals: it plates out and the purification system never takes it. In the accident it leaves with the circulating pool and the lift-off share (walk 2).

What this walk does not show: whether real silver release follows this model. Silver transport through SiC is not well understood (grain-boundary and palladium-assisted paths are not in the model), and nothing here is compared with a measured release.
