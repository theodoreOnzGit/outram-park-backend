Boundaries move, defects gather

Fission products

The grains of lesson 10 are not fixed. In hot fuel the boundaries move.

Grains coarsening

An illustrative Potts model, written for this page and run in a Web Worker: a teaching model of coarsening, not UO2 kinetics.

Predict first. Left alone and hot, do the grains get smaller, stay the same, or get bigger?

Boundaries cost energy, so they shrink

Atoms at a boundary sit badly, so a boundary stores energy. The material lowers that energy by having less boundary: atoms hop from small grains onto bigger neighbours, small grains vanish, and the average grain grows. Hotter means faster, because the hops are thermally activated (lessons 8 and 9). Fuel codes model this: SCIANTIX’s grain growth is temperature-driven, with options that slow it with burnup or stop it at a limiting size (Pizzocri et al., 2018), grain_growth.rst.

Vacancies gather into voids

Vacancies wander too (lesson 9). When they meet, they can cluster, and a large cluster is a small empty void, a place where gas atoms can collect. How this happens in UO2: source pending.

Learn more advanced stuff

The model in the demo

Each site carries a grain label. A site tries to take a random neighbour’s label; if that does not add boundary, it does; if it does, it may still, with probability exp(−ΔE/kT′). That is the Potts model, a standard teaching model of coarsening. The temperature here is in model units, not kelvin.

Why it matters for release

A moving boundary sweeps through the grain and can collect what lies in its path. Lesson 22 takes that up.
