The wall gets thinner

Fission products

Lessons 33 and 34 treated the SiC wall as fixed. In an accident it is not. Three things eat at it, and a thinner or weaker wall fails sooner.

Three ways to lose the wall

boon-lay fuel failure: advance_thinning_factor, thermal_decomposition_failure_fraction, corroded_weibull_modulus. HTR-10’s 35 µm SiC; an extrapolation.

Predict first. After 200 h at 1600 °C, has corrosion eaten 1 %, 10 % or half of the wall?

1. Corrosion: the wall is eaten from inside

Fission products attack the SiC at a rate that grows steeply with temperature (an Arrhenius law, PANAMA-I Eq (7)). The code tracks a thinning factor, FKOR = original thickness / thickness now, and multiplies the stress of lesson 33 by it. After 200 h at 1600 °C, FKOR = …: about one per cent. It matters much more at 2000 °C and above.

An honest note from the code: the corrosion rate the report prints does not reproduce the report’s own plot of the same equation; a pre-factor ten times smaller does, to digitising accuracy. boon-lay uses the smaller one and keeps the printed one beside it (corrosion.rs’s docs).

2. Decomposition: the wall turns to vapour

Above about 2000 °C the SiC decomposes into silicon vapour and graphite. The layer does not burst; it stops being a pressure vessel. The code accumulates how much decomposition has happened (an “action integral”) and reads a failure fraction φ2 off it (Eqs (11)–(14b)). This part is unverified: no figure or table of the report checks it, and the code says so.

3. Grain-boundary attack: off by default

Fission products can also attack along the boundaries between SiC grains. That does not thin the wall; it widens the scatter in its strength, lowering the Weibull modulus m of lesson 34 (Eqs (10b), (10c)). The report does not take it into account by default, and neither does boon-lay. In lesson 36 you can switch it on and see what it does.

Where it is in the code

All three are stepped by AccidentHistory::step, one time interval at a time at that interval’s temperature: advance_thinning_factor, advance_action_integral, and, if switched on, advance_grain_boundary_exposure.

Why the decomposition term is read, not added up

φ1 is added up step by step from positive increments, so a burst particle stays burst when things cool. φ2 is read straight off the action integral at each step. The two agree on a heat-up and differ on anything that cools. The theory book explains this and the two calibrations of the decomposition law (one for loose particles, one for particles inside a fuel sphere; the report’s own Fig. 6 rules out the first): step 4 and step 7.

Units the report does not print

The decomposition rate’s 375 must be a speed in m/s for the equation to balance; the report does not say so. boon-lay records each such reading in docs/panama-i-units-and-open-questions.md.
