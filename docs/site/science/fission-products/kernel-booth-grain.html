Booth on one grain

Fission products

Take one grain and pretend it is a sphere. Fission products are born evenly through it and wander (lesson 8). Any that reach the surface are gone. How much has left after a time t?

Predict first. Double the grain’s radius. To release the same fraction, does it take twice as long, or four times?

Only D t / a² matters

Booth’s answer depends on the diffusion coefficient D, the time t and the radius a only through one combination, D t / a² (boon-lay’s TRISO_ATOPS_DERIVATION.md, §3–4). Double the radius and it takes four times as long. Early on, the release grows as the square root of time: only atoms near the surface have got out.

The formula

For a stable species released from a sphere with its surface held at zero, the released fraction is f = 1 − (6/π²) Σn exp(−n²π²Dt/a²)/n², and at short times f ≈ 6√(Dt/πa²) − 3Dt/a² (derivation §4, citing Crank). The page’s JavaScript evaluates the same series, and check-fission.js checks it against boon-lay’s values.

Why the page's walk sits slightly low

The demo’s walk takes fixed time steps and only checks for escape at the end of each, so an atom that pokes out and back within a step is missed. boon-lay’s Walk-on-Spheres engine does not have this bias.
