Lump by chemistry and volatility

Fission products

Fission makes dozens of elements, but diffusion data exist for only a few. The working answer: assume elements with similar chemistry move alike, and give them one Arrhenius law between them.

A second axis: volatility

Moving through the solid is one thing; what happens once out is another. A volatile species can travel as vapour through porosity and cracks, and stick (plate out) on cooler surfaces further on. In TRISO-ATOPS’s own routing, every group plates out except the noble gases (…, marked ‘~’ in the table). Light-water reactor source terms group the elements by release behaviour instead: NUREG-1465’s Table 3.8 (Soffer et al., 1995), p. 10 (colour by “NUREG-1465 group”).

Where lumping fails

  • Pd shares Ag’s D, but palladium also attacks SiC chemically, which a diffusion coefficient cannot say: source pending for the attack.
  • Eu sits with Sr and Ba, a lanthanide grouped with alkaline earths.
  • Te and Se sit with the halogens and the noble gases; TRISO-ATOPS calls the group “halogens” but includes the chalcogens (its ElementGroup documentation says so).
  • The groups are partly set by data: the elements with measured coefficients get their own class; the rest get a nominal value. A class is a statement about data as much as about chemistry.
