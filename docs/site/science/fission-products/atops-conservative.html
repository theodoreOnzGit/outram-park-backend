Is TRISO-ATOPS’s analytical solution conservative?

Fission products

TRISO-ATOPS never solves the diffusion equation through a particle. It evaluates exact formulas on simpler shapes (Way 1). It is often said that this is done conservatively: that the simple shapes release at least as much as the real particle would. That is a claim that can be checked. This lesson checks it with Way 2: boon-lay’s mesh through all five layers of the HTR-10 particle, with the same diffusion coefficients the formulas use, so that any difference is the formulas’ assumptions and nothing else.

What TRISO-ATOPS actually computes in an accident

Read from the port (release_activity and release_fraction_transient, upstream readings), for the kernel’s inventory:

  • Caesium, strontium (and the other special metals): released = inventory × failed fraction × Booth’s F on the bare kernel with ∫D dt. So a failed particle is a bare kernel, and an intact particle releases exactly zero.
  • Silver: released = inventory × 1 (every particle) × a planar time-lag formula (Crank, 1975) through the SiC, the “breakthrough” model: steady flow through a flat SiC layer fed at the kernel’s concentration over the kernel’s area, never depleted.
  • Krypton and the other noble gases use an empirical fit, not a diffusion solution, so there is nothing here to compare it with (and boon-lay has no Kr D in the buffer, gh:#541) (CORRECTED 2026-11-10, gh:#638: boon-lay now has one, 10−8 m²/s from IAEA-TECDOC-978; the empirical fit is the reason).

Predict first. At 1600 °C for 500 h, which releases more caesium per particle on average in a fuel load where one particle in 10 000 has failed: the failed ones, or the intact ones that TRISO-ATOPS counts as zero?

Which D, and why it matters

Kernel: TRISO-ATOPS’s own correlations. Silver in SiC: TRISO-ATOPS’s own, the one its breakthrough formula uses. Every other coating: boon-lay’s walker table (Jiang et al., 2023, as boon-lay cites it, no fluence), because TRISO-ATOPS has no coefficient for caesium or strontium in a coating at all: it never moves them through one. Finding 1: on a failed particle, Booth is exact

For caesium and strontium the mesh on the bare kernel reproduces TRISO-ATOPS’s Booth to … at every hold and time. That is expected: with one D for the whole sphere, Booth on ∫D dt is the solution (why). At 1600 °C, 500 h: Cs …, Sr … (mesh: …). So the formula is not an approximation. Whatever is conservative about it lies in the assumption failed = bare kernel: no coating left to slow anything.

Getting this right took two runs. On the first, strontium at 1200 °C disagreed tenfold, because Sr moves only 0.14 µm into the kernel in 500 h and the cells were 2.5 µm. The mesh was wrong, not Booth. The protocol was fixed (a finely meshed shell at the kernel surface); the criterion was not moved. Both runs are recorded in the generator.

At 1200 and 1400 °C caesium through intact SiC is negligible (10−13 and below), and there the zero is a fair simplification. So “zero from intact particles” is not conservative at 1600 °C and above for Cs, or from 1400 °C for Sr, with these coefficients. It is a modelling choice, not the analytical solution, and the result stands or falls with the coating D values. The first job, before anyone relies on it, is to check those D values against the literature.

Finding 3: for silver the formula is conservative

The breakthrough formula assumes three things in silver’s favour: the SiC is fed at the kernel’s concentration over the kernel’s area, nothing in the kernel or PyC slows silver on its way, and the source is never depleted. A second mesh run puts just those assumptions on the real geometry (kernel, buffer and IPyC well mixed, then the SiC): it releases less than the formula, and the intact five-layer particle releases less again:

So: conservative?

  • The analytical solutions themselves are exact (Booth on the integral of D, for a sphere with one D) or conservative (silver’s breakthrough), as measured here.
  • The modelling choice around them, that an intact particle releases no caesium or strontium, is not conservative at the temperatures where it matters most, with these coating coefficients.

This page does not check what TRISO-ATOPS’s own documents claim about conservatism. It measures the formulas, and both directions are reported.

What this comparison leaves out

  • One temperature for 500 h, not an accident history. Both sides take any history the same way (∫D dt for the formula, a D per step for the mesh), but the intact particle has a different D per layer, so its answer is not a function of one integral.
  • A clean start: everything in the kernel, nothing in the coatings. After years of normal operation the coatings already hold some inventory, which would raise the intact release.
  • No decay (Ag-110m loses about 6 % in 500 h), partition ratio 1 at every boundary, no fluence, no chemistry, no graphite matrix (TRISO-ATOPS treats the matrix separately, on top of both).
  • Numerical uncertainty: doubling the cells and the steps changes every 500 h value quoted by at most … relative. Early points on the rising edge move by up to 13 % and are not quoted.
  • This is verification of formulas against a solver, not validation. No measured release is compared.

Where it is in the code

  • Failed kernel: booth_transient; silver: breakthrough_model_transient; who gets which, and the zero for intact Cs and Sr: release_activity.
  • The reference: LayeredDiffusion::uniform_in_first_layers and step, boon-lay’s own, not a port (gh:#1209).

Why the breakthrough formula clamps

Its steady part grows as 3Dt/(r a) without limit, because the source never runs out; the port clamps the result to [0, 1], as upstream does. Once the clamp is reached the formula says “everything”, which is why the ratio tends to 1 at high temperature: the real particle has nearly emptied too.
