Complication 5: bursts on heating

Fission products

Booth assumes a steady temperature. Heat the fuel quickly and D jumps up (Arrhenius, lesson 7), so atoms that were creeping now rush out. And gas that was waiting in boundary bubbles (lesson 14) can be let go at once if the boundaries crack open. The release comes as a burst.

Learn more advanced stuff

With a time-varying temperature, Booth’s formula still works if D t is replaced by ∫D(T(t)) dt, the integral of D along the temperature history, because the diffusion equation only cares about that integral when D does not depend on position.
