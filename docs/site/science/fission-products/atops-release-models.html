Four models, chosen by group

  • Noble gases and halogens: an empirical correlation in the decay constant and temperature. The paper fitted it to gas releases from “designed-to-fail” particles in the AGR-3/4 experiment (p. 6). It has the R/B slope of lesson 19 left as a fitted exponent. (rb_fail_noble_gases)
  • Special metals (Cs, Sr): Booth’s equivalent sphere, the long-time form for long-lived nuclides and the R/B form for short-lived ones. The sphere’s radius is a single number between grain and kernel (lesson 18): the code uses √(2 agrain r) = … for a … kernel and … grains. (booth_longlived, booth_shortlived_fast_diffuse)
  • Silver and palladium: breakthrough through a flat SiC wall, the time-lag solution of a membrane, scaled by the kernel’s surface to volume. Shorter-lived silver isotopes are scaled down by √(λ110m/λ). (breakthrough_model)
  • Everything else: a fixed 10−5. (OTHER_METAL_RB_FAIL)

Metals then cross the graphite: the code divides their release by an attenuation factor from slab diffusion through the 4.5 mm between fuel and coolant, capped at 108 (the “Graphite hold-up” view). Gases pass through.

The floor that should not be there

Booth’s series is an infinite sum; TRISO-ATOPS stops after … terms. At small D·t/a² the missing tail does not vanish, so the release never falls below … (the “Booth floor” view; gh:#385, which measured this). For strontium at normal-operation temperatures that over-states the release: in the “R/B by group” view, the flat stretch of Sr-90’s curve at low temperature is this floor, not physics. The port keeps it, because upstream has it and the port’s contract is to agree with upstream.

Paper and code: the sphere’s radius

The paper describes the Booth radius as the root-mean-square of the kernel size and the grain size (pp. 7–8). The code computes √(2 agrain r), the geometric mean of grain size and kernel diameter: …, against … for the root-mean-square of kernel radius and grain size, or … of kernel and grain diameters. The smaller radius gives a larger release. In an accident (lesson 45) the code uses the kernel radius itself. All three follow upstream’s code; the difference is reported, not changed. Lesson 18’s note on the physical basis of this radius is still source-pending.

The derivations

The Booth sphere, its steady state and the breakthrough and graphite closed forms are derived in the theory book (step 9) and in boon-lay’s TRISO_ATOPS_DERIVATION.md. The deep dive Getting out compares them with boon-lay’s random walk.

Why silver ignores failure

The paper applies silver’s ratio to every particle, failed or not, because the coatings are not effective barriers to silver at most reactor temperatures (p. 5). That is chapter 4’s lesson 31, made into a rule.
