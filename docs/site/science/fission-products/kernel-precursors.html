Complication 4: precursors

Fission products

Decay chains (lesson 6) change the element. A Cs-137 atom may have started as Xe-137, a gas, which decays with a half-life of … (boon-lay’s decay library). For that time it moved like a gas. So the caesium you find outside may have got out as xenon, and the family it belongs to depends on when you look.

How a precursor bends the clean curve

TRISO-ATOPS can account for this with a per-nuclide parent_decay flag, set when the nuclides are selected (boon-lay’s select_nuclides; the port’s documentation records an upstream quirk that keeps a stock run from applying it to Rh-105, reproduced as upstream has it). Link back: in the many-spheres model, part of the atom’s time runs with another family’s D.

Learn more advanced stuff

The bend shown is illustrative: a share of the life at a higher D. Doing it properly means following each atom down its chain with each link’s own D and half-life, which boon-lay’s Lagrangian walker can do (each atom carries its nuclide, chapter 2’s decay Monte Carlo).
