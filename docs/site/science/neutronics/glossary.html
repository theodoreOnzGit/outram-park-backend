Neutronics glossary

Neutronics

Every term below is explained, idea first, on the page it links to. Here each gets one line, in plain words.

Compound nucleus
The short-lived nucleus formed when a neutron joins one: it later leaves by one of its exits (re-emitting a neutron, a gamma ray, splitting, …). Page 8.
Resonance
A sharp rise in a cross section where the neutron's energy matches one of the compound nucleus's allowed levels. Page 8.
Doppler broadening
The smearing of a sharp feature, such as a resonance, because the target nuclei move: lower and wider peaks in a hotter material. Page 3; page 22.
Lethargy
How far a neutron has slowed, measured on a log-energy axis: u = ln(E0/E). Page 17.
Infinitely dilute cross section
The plain average of a cross section over its resonances. Page 18.
Fluctuation integral
An average over the spread of resonance widths, used to get the average cross section right. Page 18.
Free gas
The picture of the target as lone atoms that each recoil on their own. Page 3.
Coherent elastic scattering
Diffraction off a crystal's planes of atoms, with no energy change: the source of Bragg edges. Page 21.
Scattering law, S(α, β)
A table of how likely each pair of kick and energy change is, holding all of a material's thermal physics. Page 21.
Detailed balance
The rule that gaining a given energy is less likely than losing it, by e−β. A rule S obeys, not what S is. Page 21.
Debye–Waller factor
How much the atoms' shaking weakens sharp, no-phonon scattering. Page 21.
Normal mode
A pattern in which every atom of a lattice or molecule moves in step, at one frequency. Page 23.
Phonon
One packet of a lattice vibration's energy, ℏω, counted like a particle. Page 23.
Quasiparticle
Something that behaves like a particle but exists only inside a material, as a collective motion of many atoms. Page 23.
Polarisation
A phonon's "style": how its atoms move compared with the direction the wave travels (along: longitudinal; across: transverse). Page 23.
Density of states, ρ
How many vibration modes sit at each energy: a material's vibration fingerprint. Page 24.
Recoil line
β = −α: where every collision with a free nucleus at rest lands. Maths 1.
Sum rule
On average a collision costs the neutron exactly the free-atom recoil energy, however the atom is bound. Maths 2.
Up-scatter and down-scatter
Gaining energy by absorbing a phonon already there; losing energy by making one. Maths 3.
Phonon expansion
Writing what a collision can do as a sum: 0 phonons + 1 phonon + 2 phonons + …, each with its chance. Maths 3.
Convolution
Going through every way to make a total and adding up their chances: the distribution of a sum. Maths 3½.
Short-collision-time approximation
Treating a very quick, hard collision as if the atom were free: a bell curve instead of the phonon sum. Maths 5.
Zero-point motion
The jiggle a confined atom keeps even at absolute zero: ½ℏω per mode. Maths 5b.
Effective temperature, T̄
The atoms' real average kinetic energy, thermal plus zero-point, written as a temperature; always at least the real one. Maths 5b.
Impulse approximation
Treating a hard kick as a snapshot of the atom's momentum. Maths 5g.
Coherent and incoherent scattering
Whether the waves scattered by different atoms interfere (coherent) or just add up separately (incoherent). Maths 5h.
Triplet and singlet
The neutron and a proton with spins lined up (total spin 1) or opposed (total spin 0); they scatter very differently. Maths 5i.
