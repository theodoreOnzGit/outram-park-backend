Maths 3½: what is a convolution?

Neutronics

No phonons on this page, only dice. What is the chance that two dice total 7?

Count the ways. The pairs that make 7 are (1,6), (2,5), (3,4), (4,3), (5,2), (6,1): 6 ways out of 36, so the chance is 6/36. A total of 2 has one way, (1,1); a total of 12 has one way, (6,6). The triangle of chances that peaks at 7 comes from exactly this counting.

Going through every way to make each total, and adding up their chances, is called a convolution. That is all a convolution is.

P(total = t) = Σk P(first die = k) × P(second die = t − k)

Read it aloud: for each value k the first die could show, the second die must make up the rest, t − k. Multiply the two chances and add over all k.

Flip, slide, multiply, add

The picture: flip the second die's chances back to front, and slide them along under the first. At each position, multiply the bars that overlap and add the products: that sum is one bar of the result. Sliding all the way along builds the whole triangle. Roll 10 000 times shows sampled totals landing on the counted chances: the sampling idea of the next page, in miniature.

  • Any two distributions can be convolved the same way; the dice need not be fair.
  • With smooth curves instead of bars, the sum becomes an integral, ∫ f(k) g(t − k) dk. Same idea: every way to make the total, chances multiplied, added up.

Two things to keep straight. A convolution gives the distribution of a sum of random things, not an average and not a product. And it assumes they are independent: the dice are rolled separately.

Why is this so? Back to phonons

A phonon's energy is like a die roll, drawn from the density of states ρ (page 24). Two phonons together have a total energy, and its chances are the convolution of ρ-shaped chances. How does that build a real material's scattering?
