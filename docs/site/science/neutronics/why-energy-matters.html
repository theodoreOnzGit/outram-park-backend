Why does the neutron's energy matter?

Neutronics

On page 4, the neutron's energy set its wavelength. On page 5, the angular pattern changed smoothly as the energy changed. That covers the smooth part of how nuclear data depends on energy.

But some energies are special.

Offer a quantum system an energy

Page 4 showed a wave in a box: only whole half-wavelengths fit, so only some energies are allowed, growing as n². Now offer the box an energy from outside and watch how it answers.

Predict first. Slide the offered energy slowly upward. Does the box answer a little to every energy, or a lot to a few?

A box offered an energy

An illustration: one particle in a box with hard walls, levels at n² × the lowest, each given the same width. Not a model of any nucleus.

Between levels, almost nothing happens. When the offered energy lands on an allowed level, the box takes it and the standing wave builds up. A system answers strongly when it is offered exactly the energy of one of its allowed states. That strong answer is a resonance, and the curve along the bottom is its shape: a peak at each level, with a width.

A real nucleus does the same

A slow neutron that meets a nucleus can join it. U-238 plus a neutron becomes U-239, for a moment: the compound nucleus. The neutron brings two kinds of energy with it: its kinetic energy, and the energy released when it becomes bound, which is several MeV (Krane, 1987).

If that total lands on one of the compound nucleus's allowed levels, the interaction becomes far more likely. That is a nuclear resonance. Because the binding energy already lifts the compound nucleus several MeV above its ground state, a neutron of only a few eV probes levels high up the ladder, where the levels crowd close together.

neutron energy → the compound nucleus's total energy → on an allowed level? → a resonance, or not

This box is really simplified

A real nucleus is not one particle in a box with hard walls. It is many protons and neutrons pulling on each other, with no walls at all, and the levels a slow neutron reaches are states of the whole compound nucleus, not of one particle. The box only shows why allowed levels give resonances.
