Why does a neutron bounce?

Neutronics

Begin with the easiest interaction to picture.

Elastic scattering is a collision in which the neutron and the nucleus only exchange motion. No energy goes into the nucleus's insides, and nothing is created or destroyed.

Elastic scattering: the classical picture

Treat the neutron and the nucleus as two balls on a table. In an elastic collision two things are conserved:

  • momentum, mn v = mn v′ + M V′, and
  • kinetic energy, ½ mn v² = ½ mn v′² + ½ M V′².

From those two laws alone, a neutron that hits a nucleus at rest head-on keeps the fraction

E′ / E = ( (A − 1) / (A + 1) )²

of its energy, where A is the nucleus's mass in neutron masses. The rest goes to the nucleus.

Predict first. To take energy away from a fast neutron, is a light nucleus (hydrogen) or a heavy one (uranium) better?

Collide a neutron with a nucleus

Masses: each nucleus's atomic weight ratio A (mass in neutron masses) as its ENDF/B-VIII.0 evaluation states it (Brown & others, 2018): H-1 0.9991673 (the H-1 tape in this repository is the ENDF/B-VIII.0 beta-6 file), C-12 11.89365, U-238 236.0058. The drawn sizes are illustrative, and the animation is not to time scale; the relative speeds before and after are.

Hydrogen can take almost all of a neutron's energy in one collision; uranium-238 takes at most about 1.7 %. That is why a reactor that wants slow neutrons surrounds its fuel with light nuclei (water, graphite): the idea behind moderation, which comes back in later lessons.

What this picture is not. The two-ball collision is intuition for the kinematics of scattering only. OUTRAM PARK, like OpenMC, does not model scattering as hard spheres: it samples the outgoing angle and energy from the evaluated angular and energy distributions in the nuclear data. And at thermal energies the target nucleus is not at rest, and may be bound in a molecule or crystal, which needs another treatment altogether.
