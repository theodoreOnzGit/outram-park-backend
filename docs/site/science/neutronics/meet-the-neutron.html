Meet the neutron

Neutronics

What is neutronics?

Neutronics is the study of the behaviour of neutrons in nuclear systems.

In a nuclear reactor, neutrons move through different materials and interact with atomic nuclei. A neutron may travel through a material without interacting, scatter from a nucleus, be absorbed, or cause fission.

What's going on here?

Each coloured line is one neutron's path, from where it was born to where its history ended. The colour is the neutron's energy: it changes at a collision. The marks along and at the end of a path are what happened to it:

  • Scattering (small dots along the path). The neutron hits a nucleus and survives. Its direction and energy may change: that is what makes the path zig-zag, like a random walk.
  • Absorption (red ×). The neutron is absorbed, here without fission, and its history ends.
  • Fission (yellow dot in a ring). The neutron makes a nucleus split. New neutrons are produced and begin new histories: in the demo the next neutron starts at that spot.
  • Leakage (violet ring). The neutron leaves the uranium and never comes back.

The markers are drawn as the demo draws them; the green ring is where a neutron was born. The tracks are three-dimensional and drawn from above, so a neutron that left through the top or bottom face seems to stop inside the bar.
