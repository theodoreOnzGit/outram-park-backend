Maths 5c: stiff springs, soft springs

Neutronics

How much zero-point jiggle a mode adds depends on its spring, or more exactly on one step ℏω compared with the thermal energy kT:

  • Stiff spring, step bigger than kT: high-energy vibrations. The quantum jiggle dominates the thermal jiggle, and T̄ ends up well above T.
  • Soft spring, step smaller than kT: low-energy vibrations. They behave almost classically: thermal jiggle dominates, and T̄ ≈ T.

Stiff or soft?

A real crystal has both, soft and stiff, in proportions set by its structure: that is its density of states ρ (pages 24–25). LEAPR combines them into one effective temperature, weighting each vibration by ρ. For Si in α-quartz at 293.6 K, OUTRAM PARK's port of LEAPR gives 1.731 times the real temperature, about 508 K: quartz's stiff modes add a lot.

Deeper: the formula

One oscillator contributes (β/2) coth(β/2) T to T̄, with β = ℏω/kT (NJOY2016 manual, discrete oscillators). For a small step (soft) this tends to T; for a large one (stiff), to ℏω/2k. LEAPR weights it by ρ; summing (β/2)coth(β/2) over quartz's ρ reproduces the port's 1.731 to nine figures.

Conventions on these pages. β = (E′ − E)/kT is positive when the neutron gains energy; α measures the size of the kick. S is LEAPR's asymmetric scattering law (the NJOY2016 manual's script S); ENDF stores the symmetric S = eβ/2 times it (page 21).
