DBRC: let the resonance choose the partner

Neutronics

The last page drew the target's velocity from |v−V| · M(V), assuming the scattering cross section is the same whichever target the neutron meets. When is that wrong?

A cross section that is anything but flat

Take U-238's 6.67 eV resonance (page 12) and look at the elastic cross section, which is what decides the outgoing neutron of a scattering collision. Our RECONR port at 0 K gives, from the ENDF/B-VIII.0 evaluation (Brown & others, 2018):

  • a peak of 1512 b at 6.675 eV,
  • and, just below it, an interference dip where it falls to 0.048 b at 6.518 eV, more than four orders of magnitude lower.

A neutron of energy 6.5 eV can meet a nucleus at relative energies spread over a window of about ±0.2 eV about its own (at 293.6 K, U-238; the widget shades it). The nuclei that put the encounter on the peak are rare in the gas, but the cross section is thousands of times larger there. They should be chosen far more often than the crowd-and-relative-speed rule says.

Predict first: the neutron sits at 6.5 eV, just under the peak, where the 0 K elastic cross section is tiny. Does the correct draw move the typical encounter energy up toward the peak, or down away from it? By a lot, or a little?

The correction: accept in proportion to σs at 0 K

The right density for the target, given that the neutron scatters, is

p(V) ∝ |v − V| · M(V) · σs0 K(Erel)

with Erel the encounter energy of page 3. Doppler Broadening Rejection Correction (DBRC) gets it by rejection: draw a target as on the last page, work out its Erel, and accept it with probability σs0 K(Erel)/σmax, where σmax is the largest 0 K value over the window of relative energies the gas can reach. A rejected target is redrawn.

Two points that are easy to get wrong:

  • It is the 0 K cross section on purpose. The target's motion is now being sampled explicitly; putting the broadened cross section here would count the Doppler broadening twice.
  • The rate still comes from the broadened table. BROADR fixed how often the neutron scatters (pages 22 and 42). DBRC only changes which nucleus that scatter was off, and therefore the neutron's outgoing energy. It is transport physics, not NJOY processing, and it needs the 0 K elastic data kept alongside the broadened table.

U-238 at 6.67 eV, with and without DBRC

Top: RECONR's 0 K elastic cross section (orange; what DBRC reads) and the same reaction broadened to 293.6 K (grey, dashed; the rate), both from OUTRAM PARK's own processing of U-238, ENDF/B-VIII.0 (Brown & others, 2018). Bottom: the density of encounter energies that each sampler draws from, grey for plain sampling and orange with the rejection, by numerical integration (next to the peak the rejection accepts so few candidates that sampling enough of them would stall a phone; the check on this page confirms the real samplers land on these densities bin by bin). Dashed vertical lines mark the means. Axes are linear.

At 6.5 eV and 293.6 K the mean encounter energy moves from 6.5003 to 6.5271 eV, a shift of 27 meV up toward the peak. Those two means are numerical integrals of the two densities; the check on this page confirms that 200 000 sampled targets of each kind land on them within the sampling error. The size of the shift is small beside the eV scale, but the 6.67 eV peak is only 24 meV wide at 0 K (page 22), so it is large beside the resonance. Move the neutron closer to the peak or heat the material and watch the orange histogram grow a tail toward it.

The price is paid in rejections. At 6.5 eV and 293.6 K only 4.2 candidates in 10 000 are accepted, because σmax is the 0 K peak and the typical target sits in the dip. The transport code needs one target per collision, and its loop is unbounded, as upstream: it terminates with probability one whenever σ0 K is positive in the window. Right on the peak (6.67 eV) it is about three in ten.

Common misconception: "DBRC is Doppler broadening done again."

No. BROADR averages the cross section over the target motion to give the rate. DBRC leaves the rate alone and corrects the draw of the target that makes the collision's kinematics. Neither replaces the other, and neither belongs to S(α,β), which is a different treatment for a different energy range (page 45).

What our code does

  • On by default. A nuclide built from an ENDF tape carries the 0 K elastic table and applies DBRC unless a caller removes it with without_dbrc (nuclide.rs; pinned by tests/correct_physics_is_default.rs). OpenMC, whose algorithm this is, treats resonance scattering as an option that a user switches on (Romano et al., 2015).
  • The window. Erel is limited to Eup = (√(AE/kT) + 4)2 kT/A, four thermal speeds above the neutron's; candidates above it are redrawn; σmax is taken over [Elow, Eup]. The rejection loop is unbounded, as upstream (scatter.rs:500-565; OpenMC physics.cpp:870-965).
  • The energy range. Applied from 0.01 eV up to 1 keV by default (DBRC_DEFAULT_E_MAX_EV = 1000); above 1 keV a nuclide with a table is treated as at rest. Nuclides with no 0 K table follow the 400 kT rule of page 42.
  • Where the 0 K data comes from. On the ENDF route, from RECONR at 0 K (process::cross_sections keeps it as elastic_0k, on by default). A continuous-energy ACE file made at a temperature carries only the broadened elastic column, so on the ACE route the 0 K data must come from a companion 0 K ACE file of the same nuclide (Nuclide::with_elastic_0k_from_ace), and a hot table alone leaves DBRC off.
Deeper: two defects the checks have already caught in this sampler

Both are recorded in the crate's own history. Until 2026-09-29 the sampler tested the 400 kT rule first, so DBRC never acted above 10.1 eV and U-238's 20.9 and 36.7 eV resonances were scattered off nuclei at rest; and its window was about half as wide as upstream's (verification_and_validation/ace_route_physics/urr_dbrc_sab_as_openmc_2026-09-29.md). Until 2026-09-30 the rejection loop was capped at 4096 trials and returned an unaccepted candidate when the cap was hit, which undid most of the correction next to a resonance; a single-scatter test at 20.5 eV against OpenMC gave χ2/ν = 2625/59 on that version. The importance-weighting test that now guards the sampler (DBRC density equals the plain density times σ0 K) has a worst |z| of 2.65 over 30 bins.

Does it matter for keff?

The physical effect is the one in the widget: more encounters on the resonance means more up-scattering into the resonance's energy range, more capture in U-238, and a slightly lower keff. How big the effect is in our code is not well resolved, and this is everything that is recorded:

Next question
