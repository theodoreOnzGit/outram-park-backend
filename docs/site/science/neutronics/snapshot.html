Maths 5d: what does a hard kick see?

Neutronics

A hard kick is over almost instantly. The atom is kicked before its springs can push back, so the neutron only "sees" how fast the atom happened to be moving at that moment: a snapshot of the atom's motion.

Energy lost = free recoil + a small shift from the atom's motion. If the atom was moving toward the kick, the neutron loses a little more; if away, a little less. The shift depends on the atom's speed along the kick: the Doppler-like idea of page 22 and rung 1½ again.

This picture is the big-kick limit. For gentle kicks the atom does feel its springs, which gives the elastic spike and the lopsided Poisson of rungs 3–4.
