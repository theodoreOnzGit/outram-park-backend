Maths 5f: what sets the width?

Neutronics

The width comes from how fast the atoms really move, that is, their kinetic energy:

width² ≈ 2 × recoil × (jiggle energy)

For a free gas this is exact: in rung 2's formula the spread of β is √(2α), so the variance of the energy change is 2·ER·kT. For bound atoms it holds in the big-kick limit, with the effective temperature in place of T.

The effective temperature T̄ is simply the atoms' real average kinetic energy, thermal plus zero-point, written as a temperature.

Width from jiggle energy

Common misconception: "T̄ is just a fitting fudge."

No: it is the atoms' actual average kinetic energy, thermal plus zero-point, expressed as a temperature. Nothing in it is fitted.
