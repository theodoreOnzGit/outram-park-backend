Can we skip the gamma paths?

Neutronics

Page 13's exact theory was unaffordable, mostly because of the gamma channels. Which part can we drop with the least damage? The gamma paths.

Reich and Moore built their formula for a compound nucleus with one neutron channel and a large number of channels for capture (Reich & Moore, 1958). They do not track the gamma channels one by one. They eliminate them:

  • every gamma channel of a level is lumped into one total capture width Γγ per level, the "eliminated width" (MacFarlane et al., 2017);
  • that width enters only as an imaginary part of the level's energy, Er − iΓγ/2, so it widens the level without needing a channel of its own;
  • the neutron channel and up to two fission channels keep full multi-level interference.

So the matrix shrinks from thousands of channels to the few that are left: at most 3 × 3 per spin group (neutron plus two fission channels), and just a number, 1 × 1, for a nucleus that does not fission. Capture is whatever is left: total minus elastic minus fission (Trkov et al., 2018).

Things to try:

  • Capture, same J. Capture is not the sum of two peaks either. Reich and Moore showed it: without fission, their capture cross section "differs formally from a sum of single-level Breit-Wigner terms" (Reich & Moore, 1958). The interference is in the neutron channel, and capture feels it.
  • Add a fission width, then Fission, same J. Two fission channels, two levels: the fission amplitudes interfere too. That is what Reich and Moore built the formula for.
  • Different J. Again no interference, in any channel.

Who uses it

ENDF-102 recommends Reich–Moore for all evaluations: it is "a true multilevel formalism", and all its cross sections are non-negative (Trkov et al., 2018). Of the 51 ENDF/B-VIII.0 neutron evaluations in this repository, 14 give their first resolved range in it (LRF = 3): Al-27, Si-28, -29 and -30, Ti-48, Cr-50, -52, -53 and -54, Mn-55, Fe-56 and -58, and both U-235 and U-238. That includes the U-238 level of page 12.

The general version: R-Matrix Limited

Newer evaluations use a general form with the same gamma trick. ENDF's R-Matrix Limited format (LRF = 7) "contains all the generality of LRF=3 plus unlimited numbers and types of channels", charged-particle exits included (Trkov et al., 2018), and it still keeps an "eliminated" channel for the radiation width (App. D.1.7). NJOY's manual calls it "Reich-Moore-Limited": its R-matrix is the "Reich-Moore 'eliminated width' approximation", and it allows inelastic scattering, Coulomb (charged-particle) reactions and resonance angular distributions (MacFarlane et al., 2017). 4 of our evaluations use it: Fe-54, Fe-57, Cu-63 and Cu-65. It is page 13's brute force, kept for everything except the gamma rays.

Deeper: the Reich–Moore level matrix

For each spin group, over the neutron channel n and the fission channels f1, f2 (ENDF-102 App. D.1.3, eq. D.28):

(I − K)nb = δnb − (i/2) Σr Γnr½ Γbr½ / (Er − E − iΓγr/2)

With ρ = I − (I − K)−1, the cross sections are, summed over the spin groups with their gJ:

σabs = (4π/k²) gJ [ Re ρnn − |ρnn|² ]   and   σf = (4π/k²) gJ [ |ρnf1|² + |ρnf2|² ]

and capture is σabs − σf. The gamma channels appear only in Γγr, in the denominator. The (I − K) matrix is at most 3 × 3; without fission it is one number. NJOY inverts the 3 × 3 complex matrix in its frobns routine.

Why is this so? Can we simplify further?

Reich–Moore is accurate enough for the fissile actinides, and a small matrix. But it is still a complex matrix inversion, at every energy, for every spin group.
