Maths 5g: checking it, one spring then a crystal

Neutronics

One spring. A quantum swing's average kinetic energy, thermal plus zero-point, gives

kT̄ = (ℏω/2) coth(ℏω/2kT)

which is exactly the NJOY2016 manual's term (β/2) coth(β/2) T with β = ℏω/kT. Soft spring (ℏω ≪ kT): T̄ → T, essentially classical. Stiff spring (ℏω ≫ kT): kT̄ → ℏω/2, zero-point dominated. That is rung 5c's result, now derived.

One spring's effective temperature

The whole crystal. Weight every vibration by the density of states ρ (rung 5c). For Si in α-quartz at 293.6 K, summing (β/2)coth(β/2) over its ρ gives 1.731, the same effective-temperature ratio OUTRAM PARK's port of LEAPR computes, to nine figures (checked on every build).

Treating a hard kick as a snapshot of the atom's momentum is called the impulse approximation. LEAPR's short-collision-time formula (rung 5) uses this idea.

The rung 5 group in one card. Centre: the free recoil, set by the kick and the mass (5a). Bell shape: the atoms' speeds are bell-shaped, thermally and quantum-mechanically (1½, 5b, 5e). Width: the atoms' real jiggle energy, the effective temperature T̄ (5b, 5c, 5f).
