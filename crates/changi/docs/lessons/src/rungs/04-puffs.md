## The problem

A release lasts twenty minutes, and halfway through the wind swings from east
to north. Rung 1's plume has no time in it and one wind direction, so it can
represent neither fact. **How do we follow a release that starts, stops and
meets a wind that turns?**

---

## Step 1. Chop the release into puffs

**Short answer.** Emit a **puff** every `puff_dt`, each carrying the mass
released in that interval. Carry each puff on the wind, let it spread as it
travels, and add up every live puff's contribution at the receptor.

**The formula.** One puff of mass `Q`, centred at `(x_p, y_p, H)`, is a
three-dimensional Gaussian with its ground image (rung 1's reflection):

```text
C = Q / ((2 pi)^{3/2} sigma_y^2 sigma_z)
    * exp(-((x_r - x_p)^2 + (y_r - y_p)^2) / (2 sigma_y^2))
    * [ exp(-(z_r - H)^2 / (2 sigma_z^2)) + exp(-(z_r + H)^2 / (2 sigma_z^2)) ]
```

with `sigma` evaluated at the distance the puff has **travelled** (rung 2's
`changi` set). Compare it with the plume:

- **There is no wind speed in it.** A puff is a snapshot; the wind enters only
  through where the puff is and how far it has come. Upstream's kernel
  declares a `U` argument and never reads it; the port does not take it.
- **The horizontal spread is circular.** `sigma_y` serves for both `x` and
  `y`: along-wind spreading is represented by the **spacing** of successive
  puffs, so giving each puff a `sigma_x` as well would count it twice.
- **An unmoved puff reports zero.** The sigmas are undefined at zero distance
  and upstream maps the result to 0. A modelling artefact, not physics, and
  the doc comment says so.

**Animation.** [In the demo](../../../demos/dispersion/?rung=puffs&step=single-puff): a single puff drifting and growing; then
[the rung view](../../../demos/dispersion/?rung=puffs) runs a train of them merging into
something that looks like a plume.

## Step 2. A train in a steady wind is (almost) a plume

**Short answer.** Almost. A puff at distance `x_p` carries `sigma(x_p)`, not
`sigma(x_r)`, so the sum over puffs is a smeared version of the plume. A
second-order expansion predicts the size of the difference, and the measured
difference matches the prediction.

**The check.** `crates/buangkok/tests/changi_puff_train_vs_plume.rs`
(gh:#380), Gate 2, constant wind, fixed class, 40 m stack, ground-level
centreline: the ratio `R` of the puff train's `chi/Q` to pyDOSEIA's plume
(fed `changi`'s own sigmas) must track the predicted residual
`c2 = (sigma_y^2 P)'' / (2 P)` inside a validity domain fixed before the run.
Recorded 2026-09-29 in the test's module doc: **all 21 points in the domain
pass**, for example

|---|---|---|
| D, 5.0 km | -6.878e-4 | -6.876e-4 |
| C, 2.5 km | -8.934e-4 | -8.984e-4 |
| B, 0.8 km | -7.022e-3 | -7.118e-3 |
| A, 1.5 km | +5.393e-2 | +5.415e-2 |

**Re-run for this lesson** (`develop` `d4428668be`, one core (core 12, `-j 1`), 2026-10-04, 16.1 s for the file's four tests): all

This is **verification** of the puff train against an independent model in

## Step 3. A wind that turns: puffs that remember

**Short answer.** In upstream R `puff`, a puff flies a straight line on the
wind of its birth for its whole life and never turns. The port's default
treats each puff as a **parcel**: its position is the integral of the wind it
has actually met, and its dispersion distance is the **path length** it has
travelled.

**The formula.**

```text
upstream (frozen wind):   x_p = x_0 + u_emit * age,      distance = |x_p - x_0|  (chord)

port default (Lagrangian): x_p(t + dt) = x_p(t) + u(t) dt,  distance += |u(t)| dt  (path)
```

On a constant wind the two agree algebraically (`n` steps of `u dt` is
`u * age`), so no steady-wind number moves; they differ on every other wind.

**Animation.** [In the demo](../../../demos/dispersion/?rung=puffs): run the train, then turn the wind
90°. Under the default the old puffs keep their place and the train bends;
tick "frozen wind" (upstream's rule) and every puff keeps flying the bearing
it was born on. (Both policies side by side in one view is not built:
[gh:#555](https://github.com/theodoreOnzGit/outram-park-backend/issues/555).)

## Step 4. The second stability class: one puff, not two

**Short answer.** One puff, with the whole mass, dispersed with the more
unstable class. Upstream does two contradictory things: its kernel silently
**drops** the second class, and its drivers **duplicate** the puff, one copy
per class, **each with the full mass**.

**The measurement.** Across the four Singapore monsoon regimes in
`puff::climatology`, at 14:00 and 02:00, 50 m downwind, upstream's peak
divided by the mass-conserving one runs from **1.04 to 3.97**, so an upstream
result cannot be corrected by dividing by two. The port's default is
`EmissionPolicy::OnePuffPerEmission`; upstream's is
`EmissionPolicy::UpstreamRecycleStabilityClasses`, asked for by name. Details:
[the puff-train chapter](../puff-train.md#defects-2-and-3-the-second-stability-class).

**The pattern.** Both defaults follow the workspace rule that the physics the
model is meant to represent is on by default, and reproducing upstream is an
explicit, visible choice.

## Step 5. What the puff model still does not do

These limitations are recorded in issues; the lesson states them rather than
hiding them.

| Limitation | Where it bites | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| Upstream froze each puff's wind at emission | Fixed in `changi` by the Lagrangian default; `htgr_sim_v1`'s map had its own version of the defect | #344 |
| A map that re-derived **one** stability class from the current wind and applied it to every puff aloft | `htgr_sim_v1`'s map only: in `changi::puff::simulate` each puff stores its own class at emission (`Puff::class`), so the defect is not in this crate | #352 |
| One fixed wind regime on the map; direction only rotates the plume | the map's design decision; no direction–speed–stability correlation, no calms, no meander, no plume rise | #384 |
| Day/night from a fixed clock window; a run keeps its start hour | `RunConfig::start_hour` holds the hour for the whole run (upstream re-derives it per timestamp); identical inside one day/night block, different across 07:00 or 19:00, and stated rather than verified | — |
| Sigma fits empirical over about 0.1–10 km, applied everywhere | everywhere | — |
| Output in ppm **of methane** from the drivers | `simulate_*` keep upstream's unit for the code-to-code comparison; the kernel returns kg/m³, and `activity` (rung 5) works per unit mass | — |
| Grid mode never writes its last output row; sensor mode averages, grid mode is instantaneous | upstream behaviour, reproduced and pinned by tests | — |

**Side path: the Lagrangian random walk.** A puff is an analytic answer to
"where does a cloud of particles go?". The other answer is to release
**computational particles** and move each one by the wind plus a random
turbulent kick: a Monte Carlo random walk, the same idea as the neutron
transport in the [Monte Carlo deep dive](../../monte-carlo/index.html). That
is FLEXPART, ported into `changi::flexpart` and verified kernel by kernel
against the compiled Fortran, but **deferred** (gh:#410): it cannot yet read
meteorology and drives nothing. [Side path: the random walk
(coming)](./side-random-walk.md).

**Predict (into rung 5).** Everything so far is per unit mass. A real release
is twenty nuclides, some with half-lives of minutes. Do we need twenty puff
runs, one per nuclide?

## Deliberate liberties

| Liberty | Whose | What it may cost |
|---|---|---|
| Circular puffs (`sigma_x = sigma_y`) | upstream | Along-wind spread only through puff spacing |
| Forward Euler advection on the wind at the start of each step | the port | First order in `sim_dt`; exact on a constant wind; 10 s steps against winds changing on minutes |
| Puffs dropped after `puff_duration` (1200 s default) | upstream | A receptor beyond the reach reads only a negligible Gaussian tail (1.06e-19 Bq·s/m³ at 8 km in rung 5's example); `activity` guards against an all-zero answer (rung 5) |
| Start hour held for the whole run | the port | Differs from upstream across a day/night boundary; not covered by the fixture |
