## The problem

A continuous release lasts a year. In that year the wind blows from every
direction, at every speed, in every stability class. A single steady plume
cannot represent that. **How does a long-term dilution factor average a year
of weather?**

pyDOSEIA's answer, ported in
[`buangkok::pydoseia::met`](../../../api/buangkok/pydoseia/met/index.html), is
the classical one: count the hours.

## The triple joint frequency distribution (TJFD)

Every hourly record carries a stability class (A–F), a wind speed (km/h, as
upstream's input column) and a direction. The TJFD counts the hours in each
cell of

```text
6 stability classes  x  10 speed classes  x  16 direction sectors (22.5 deg each)
```

with speed-class edges `WSRANGE_KMPH` and representative speeds
`WSPEED_K_KMPH` (upstream's values, kept). Speed class 0 is **calm**.

The steps, in the order upstream runs them:

1. **Pre-processing** (`preprocess_records`): gap filling and the hour filter
   to the operating window. A known upstream quirk, kept: the operating hours
   are `|start - end|` while the filter keeps both ends, so a 6–18 h window
   keeps 13 hourly values per day but counts 12 in the non-calm-hours
   denominator.
2. **Counting** (`tjfd_from_records`). A record with a missing speed (999) but
   a valid direction and class falls outside every speed class and is
   dropped from the TJFD.
3. **Missing-data correction** (`missing_correction`): each count scaled by
   `1 + invalid / valid` and **truncated to an integer**, as upstream's
   `astype(int)` does.
4. **Calm correction** (`calm_correction_factors`): calm hours have no
   direction, so they are shared out over the sectors by
   `1 + N_0 N_JL / (N_L N_J)`.

**Defect D1.** The formula's `N_L` is the total in the lowest non-calm speed
class over all sectors. Upstream computes the total **in direction sector 1
over all speed classes**. The port reproduces it (`CalmCorrection::Upstream`)
and offers the corrected reading of the formula as a labelled divergence
(`CalmCorrection::LowestSpeedClassTotal`), **not checked against the
Hukkoo–Bapat manual** upstream cites, which was not available. A mutation test
shows the code-to-code fixture rejects the corrected factors, i.e. the fixture
can tell them apart.

## From counts to a dilution factor

[`dilution_long_term_with_met`](../../../api/buangkok/pydoseia/dispersion/fn.dilution_long_term_with_met.html)
sums rung 1's **sector-averaged** master equation over classes and speed
classes, weighted by the counts:

```text
chi/Q (sector J) = [ sum over classes i, speed classes k >= 1 of
                       pre_i * expo_i * N_iJk / (u_k * factor_i) ]  /  (non-calm hours)
                   x (calm-correction factor of J, optionally),   averaged over the years
```

`u_k` is the speed class's representative speed (converted to m/s here only)
and `factor_i` rung 1's height correction.

## The single-plume mode with met data (defect D3)

For a short release with met data, upstream divides the six per-class
dilution factors by each class's mean speed (`speed_distribution`, the 0.90
quantile). It fails: the division broadcasts a `(6,)` array to `(6, 6)` and
trips upstream's own shape assertion, and the means are in **km/h** while the
dilution factor is per 1 m/s (a factor 3.6). The mode **cannot run upstream**.
The port's driver keeps that behaviour
(`AssessmentError::SinglePlumeWithMetCannotRun`), and
`dilution_single_plume_with_met_speeds` is the corrected, labelled divergence,
with a unit test and no upstream to compare against.
