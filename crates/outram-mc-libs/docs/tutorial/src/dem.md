HTR-10 $k_\text{eff}$ on that page was computed on pebbles stacked in a
regular lattice. The real core is a heap of pebbles poured in from the top.

**The question of this page:** *what does a poured bed look like, how is one
computed, and how does it differ from the lattice the $k$ runs use?*

*The demo pours pebbles into the HTR-10 vessel in your browser, in a

full core; the screen says so. In a desktop browser on the machine where this
page was written, the pour settled after about 5 minutes (22 406 steps, about
10 ms per step on a loaded machine; 2026-10-07).*

## 1. How is a pebble bed computed?

**Answer.** By the *discrete element method* (DEM). Each pebble is a sphere
with a position, a velocity and a spin. At every time step the code finds
which pebbles touch, computes the contact force on each pair from how far
they overlap and how fast they slide, adds gravity, and moves every pebble
forward by one step of 35 µs. A pour of 27 554 pebbles takes 16 000 such
steps to come to rest.

The workspace's DEM is
[`outram-park-fork-liggghts`](https://github.com/theodoreOnzGit/outram-park-backend/tree/@@COMMIT@@/crates/outram-park-fork-liggghts),
a Rust port of the LIGGGHTS discrete-element code. Its `GranularSystem`
engine reproduces upstream LIGGGHTS bit for bit on single contacts, and to
four decimals in packing fraction on the full HTR-10 core. That is a
comparison with another code, so it is **verification**, not validation: no
measured bed is compared anywhere in the repository.

The demo's worker calls `Htr10Fill::advance` once per chunk of steps
([`engine.rs`](https://github.com/theodoreOnzGit/outram-park-backend/blob/@@COMMIT@@/crates/dhoby-ghaut/examples/dem_web/engine.rs)).
Here is how that call reaches the contact law. The friction part (the
Coulomb limit $|F_t| \le \mu |F_n|$, with the shear spring that lets a
static heap hold its shape) is in `resolve_pure`:

## 2. Which settings, and why

The pour uses the settings of the HTR-10 filling-fraction study
([gh:#216](https://github.com/theodoreOnzGit/outram-park-backend/issues/216)),
which are the defaults of `Htr10FillSettings`:

| setting | value | where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| sliding friction µ | 0.1 | graphite on graphite is reported at 0.1–0.2 (graphite is a solid lubricant); **an assumption with no specific paper cited**, ablated over 0.1–0.4, not fitted |
| rolling friction µ_r | 0 | ablated against 0.1 |
| Young's modulus E | 5 × 10⁸ Pa | softened from graphite's ~9 GPa, set by measuring the contact overlap, not by its effect on packing |
| ν, e, ρ | 0.2, 0.5, 1730 kg/m³ | graphite |
| time step | 35 µs | 11.7 % of the Rayleigh time at this E |
| vessel | 0.90 m core radius, 0.369 m conus, 0.25 m tube | the published HTR-10 geometry; the conus is a triangle mesh |

## 3. The full-size bed, and its filling fraction

**Prediction, written before the run** (posted on
[#787](https://github.com/theodoreOnzGit/outram-park-backend/issues/787),
2026-10-07): re-running the gh:#216 command on 3 threads instead of 12 would
give a bed **byte-identical** to the committed one, settled at 16 000 steps,
with a whole-core filling fraction of 0.6047, in 4–7 minutes.

**Result** (2026-10-07, `8df5cde5fb`; hardware: i9-13900K, 32 logical CPUs
of which 16 visible to the run, 3 threads used, 62 GB RAM, Linux 7.2.7, CPU
only, shared with other jobs at load average 9–15): the bed and
its packing-fraction trace are **byte-identical** to the committed files.
It settled at **16 000 steps** (kinetic energy 3.6 × 10⁻⁴ of a one-radius
drop) in **356 s**, about 22 ms per step: inside the predicted time, slower
per step than predicted (15–20 ms).

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| pebbles | 27 554 (25 097 above the conus) |
| whole-core filling fraction $N V / (\pi R^2 h)$ | **0.6047** |
| published (IAEA-TECDOC-1382) | 0.61, quoted from a specification table, not measured |
| difference | −0.9 % |
| bulk slab (4 radii from the floor and the surface excluded) | 0.6083 |

Reaching a quoted figure is **not validation**. What the gh:#216 study showed
is that friction, not slow recirculation, sets the packing fraction: µ from
0.4 to 0.1 is worth +0.0196. The record is
[`htr10_dem_bed_bake/`](https://github.com/theodoreOnzGit/outram-park-backend/tree/@@COMMIT@@/crates/dhoby-ghaut/verification_and_validation/htr10_dem_bed_bake)
and V&V § 4.9 of the DEM crate.

## 4. The reduced pour in the demo

The demo pours fewer pebbles, so its bed is shallow: 4 000 pebbles fill the
tube and the conus and stand only about 15 cm (2.5 pebble diameters) above
the conus. Its whole-core filling fraction is therefore not comparable with
0.6047. The rough top layer counts for much of so shallow a bed, and the
measured value is 0.44.

The demo therefore also plots a **bulk** fraction. It leaves out 4 radii at
the valve and at the surface, as the DEM crate's bulk-slab measure does, and
divides by the volume of the vessel in between: tube, conus and barrel.

- **Prediction** (written after the 0.44 above had been seen, and before this
  measure was first run): 0.600–0.607 on the full-size bed, and 0.57–0.60 on
  the 4 000-pebble pour.
- **Result** (natively, one thread, same machine and load): **0.6061** on the full-size bed and
  **0.5970** on the pour (settled at 22 500 steps, 131 s, 5.8 ms per step).
  Both predictions held. The pour is lower because the narrow tube and the
  conus carry more of the wall's loose layer.
- **The browser's pour is a different random bed.** It settled at
  **0.5961** bulk and **0.4457** whole-core after 22 406 steps. WebAssembly
  computes `sin` and `cos` with a different maths library, so the random seed
  positions differ in the last bits and the chaotic pour diverges. The two
  are different random beds. Neither is wrong.

## 5. The lattice beside the random bed

*The demo cuts both beds by the same plane: the lattice that every recorded
$k$ uses (`assemble_explicit_triso(14, 12, 0)`), and the poured bed cut to
the same 16 681 balls above the floor.*

| | lattice (N = 12) | random (DEM, cut to the same count) |
|---|---|---|
| balls above the floor | 16 681 | 16 681 |
| bed surface (99th percentile + r) | 123.6 cm | 123.7 cm |
| whole-core filling fraction | 0.5999 | 0.5994 |
| $k_\text{eff}$ | recorded ([step 7](triso.md#7-the-htr-10-pebble-bed)) | **not run** |

At the same ball count the two beds stand at the same height to about a
millimetre, so the lattice does not misplace the fuel in height. What differs
is the arrangement: rows of balls with straight gaps between them in the
lattice, none in the poured bed. Neutrons can stream along straight gaps.
**What that does to $k$ has not been measured.** A smoke run in `nee_soon`
shows only that a DEM-built core transports without losing particles, and its
own record says it is not a comparison. No $k$ for the random bed is shown
here.

## Deliberate liberties on this page

| liberty | why | what it may cost |
|---|---|---|
| soft pebbles, E = 5 × 10⁸ Pa instead of ~9 GPa | a stiff contact needs a far shorter step | contacts overlap by up to ~1.7 % of the radius; set by measuring overlap, not packing |
| µ = 0.1 from a stated range, no specific paper | graphite is a solid lubricant | µ is worth 0.02 in φ (gh:#216); ablated, not fitted |
| a 25 cm stub of the discharge tube | the bed above the conus is what matters | the tube's few pebbles sit on a flat valve |
| the browser pours 4 000 pebbles, not 27 554 | one browser thread; the full pour is ~6 minutes on 3 native threads | a shallow bed; its φ is not the core's (section 4) |
| the full-size bed is a pour into the conus of a bed LIGGGHTS settled on a flat floor | that is the gh:#216 protocol | a start that is not a pour from empty; `htr10_fill`'s fresh pour exists but was not used for the full-size bake |

## Run it yourself

```text
# the full-size bed (about 6 minutes on 3 threads)
cargo run --release -p outram-park-fork-liggghts --example htr10_recirculation_sweep -- \
  --label mine_mu10_mur00 --mu 0.1 --mu-r 0 --threads 3 --trace-only
# the demo's pour, natively, one thread
cargo run --release -p dhoby-ghaut --example dem_web -- --headless 4000 40000
```

**Modify.** Pour with `--mu 0.4 --mu-r 0.1`, the crate's old setting.
Predict first: does the filling fraction go up or down, and by about how
much? Section 4.9 of the DEM crate's V&V has the answer to check against.
