## 1. Why is water such a good moderator?

**Answer.** Rung 2 needed about a hundred collisions with carbon to slow a
fission neutron to thermal. Hydrogen's nucleus has almost exactly the
neutron's mass, so a single head-on collision can stop the neutron dead.

**Formula.** For elastic scattering off a nucleus of mass ratio $A$
(isotropic in the centre of mass, target at rest), a neutron of energy $E$
leaves with an energy between $\alpha E$ and $E$, where

$$\alpha = \left(\frac{A-1}{A+1}\right)^2 .$$

On average it gains the same amount of *lethargy* $u = \ln(E_0/E)$ at
every collision:

$$\xi = 1 + \frac{\alpha \ln \alpha}{1-\alpha} .$$

So slowing from 2 MeV to 0.025 eV takes $n = \ln(2\times10^6/0.025)/\xi$
collisions. Here are those numbers, computed from each ENDF/B-VIII.0 tape's
own atomic weight ratio (AWR) on 2026-10-04:

| nuclide | AWR (tape) | $\alpha$ | $\xi$ | collisions, 2 MeV → 0.025 eV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H-1 | 0.9991673 | 1.7 × 10⁻⁷ | 0.9999973 | **18.2** |
| H-2 | 1.9968 | 0.1106 | 0.7261 | 25.1 |
| C-12 | 11.89365 | 0.7138 | 0.1591 | 114 |
| O-16 | 15.85751 | 0.7768 | 0.1210 | 150 |
| U-238 | 236.0058 | 0.9832 | 0.00845 | 2153 |

H-1's $\alpha$ is not exactly zero, because the neutron is slightly heavier
than the proton. The textbook "$\alpha = 0$, $\xi = 1$" is this number to six
figures.

**There is no function in the code that computes $\xi$.** It *emerges* from
repeating one collision. Each collision is sampled by the free-gas /
target-at-rest kernel, the same one rung 1 used:

## 2. Hydrogen in water is not a free proton

**Answer.** Below a few eV the neutron's energy is comparable to the energies
of the water molecule's vibrations and rotations. A slow neutron therefore
sees the molecule, not a lone proton. It can *gain* energy from the
molecule's motion as well as lose it, and the bound proton scatters more
strongly: about **81.8 b per hydrogen** as $E \to 0$, against **20.4 b** for a
free proton. That ratio is the bound-atom factor $((A+1)/A)^2$, quoted from
the documentation of
[`material/thermal.rs`](https://github.com/theodoreOnzGit/outram-park-backend/blob/@@COMMIT@@/crates/outram-mc-libs/src/material/thermal.rs).
The physics is described by the **thermal scattering law** $S(\alpha,\beta)$
for H in H₂O. The workspace builds it from the ENDF/B-VIII.0 tape
`tsl-HinH2O.endf` with its own port of NJOY's THERMR.

**The code forks.** At every collision with hydrogen the code takes one of
three paths:

1. below the table's cutoff, it samples the bound-atom law
   (`Nuclide::sample_thermal`);
2. otherwise, below 400 kT, it samples free gas with the target's thermal
   motion;
3. above that, it uses two-body kinematics off a nucleus at rest.

## 3. Rods in a lattice: describing repeated structure

**Answer.** The code does not describe 4961 rods one by one. It describes
**one pin** once, as a *universe*: a fuel cylinder, its clad, and water. It
then places copies of that pin on a grid, which is a *lattice*, and places
copies of the lattice on a bigger grid. LCT-008 is three levels deep:

```text
pin universe            UO₂ r < 0.514858 cm | Al-6061 clad to 0.602996 | water
  → 15 × 15 lattice, pitch 1.63576 cm                   (one assembly tile)
    → 7 × 7 core lattice, pitch 24.5364 cm              (assembly or all-water tile)
      → vacuum-bounded cylinder r < 76.200 cm, |z| < 81.662 cm
```

**Formula.** Finding which element of a lattice holds a point is integer
division:

$$i = \left\lfloor \frac{x - x_\text{ll}}{p_x} \right\rfloor ,\qquad j = \left\lfloor \frac{y - y_\text{ll}}{p_y} \right\rfloor$$

Here $x_\text{ll}, y_\text{ll}$ are the lattice's lower-left corner and
$p_x, p_y$ its pitch. The point is then moved into that element's own frame
(subtract the element's centre), and the search repeats inside the element's
universe. One trap: OpenMC writes lattice rows from the **top** down, and
this code counts from the bottom up, so the rows are reversed when they are
read.

## 4. Tracking through a lattice

**Answer.** How far can a neutron fly before it leaves its cell? The answer is
the smallest of the distances to every boundary of its cell, **at every
level**: the pin's cylinders, the lattice element's edges, the assembly's
edges. Each distance is computed in that level's own frame. If the sampled
collision distance is shorter, the neutron collides. Otherwise it moves to the
boundary, crosses it, and is located again.

$$d = \min(d_\text{collision},\ d_\text{boundary}),\qquad d_\text{collision} = -\frac{\ln \xi}{\Sigma_t}$$

## 5. How much water? Under- and over-moderation

**Answer.** More water per rod helps in one way and hurts in another. Neutrons
lose fewer of their number in the U-238 resonances while slowing down, so the
resonance escape probability $p$ rises. But more thermal neutrons are absorbed
in the water instead of the fuel, so the thermal utilisation $f$ falls.
$k_\infty = \eta f p \varepsilon$ (rung 2) therefore has a maximum. A lattice
with less water than the maximum is **under-moderated**. One with more is
**over-moderated**.

The knob is the moderator-to-fuel volume ratio:

$$\frac{V_m}{V_f} = \frac{p^2 - \pi r_\text{clad}^2}{\pi r_\text{fuel}^2}$$

For LCT-008, $V_m/V_f = 1.84$.

<div class="predict">

**Predict.** Where is the peak, and on which side of it is LCT-008? Then: what
does the dissolved boron do to your answer?

</div>

**The calculation.**
[`examples/lct008_pitch_sweep.rs`](https://github.com/theodoreOnzGit/outram-park-backend/blob/@@COMMIT@@/crates/outram-mc-libs/examples/lct008_pitch_sweep.rs)
puts one LCT-008 rod in a square cell with reflective walls, which makes it an
infinite lattice. It then computes $k_\infty$ and the factors at 11 pitches:
ENDF/B-VIII.0 at 293.6 K, H₂O S(α,β), 5000 × [50 + 200] neutrons. OpenMC ran
the same model as a cross-check. We wrote our own prediction before running:
borated peak at $V_m/V_f \approx$ 2–3, and LCT-008 slightly under-moderated.

| water | where $k_\infty$ peaks | $k_\infty$ at LCT-008's pitch | LCT-008 is |
|---|---|---|---|
| case 1, 1511 ppm boron | 1.35–1.45 cm ($V_m/V_f \approx$ 0.8–1.15), $k_\infty \approx 1.119$ | 1.06403 ± 0.00117 | **over-moderated**, ~5500 pcm below the peak |
| boron removed (ablation) | ≈ 1.75 cm ($V_m/V_f \approx 2.3$), $k_\infty \approx 1.351$ | 1.34838 ± 0.00112 | slightly under-moderated |

([`pitch_sweep.md`](https://github.com/theodoreOnzGit/outram-park-backend/blob/@@COMMIT@@/crates/outram-mc-libs/verification_and_validation/tutorial_rung4/pitch_sweep.md),
2026-10-04; the

- **Our prediction was wrong where it mattered.** The 1511 ppm of boron moves
  the peak far to the left. In that water LCT-008 is well over-moderated:
  removing water would *raise* $k_\infty$. What our prediction described was
  the clean-water lattice.
- **$p$ and $f$ behave as the formula says.** $p$ rises from 0.466 to 0.933
  across the sweep and $f$ falls from 0.878 to 0.234. $\varepsilon$ falls
  too, from 1.47 to 1.10, more than we predicted: at tight pitch, fast
  neutrons reach the next rod before they reach the water.
- **OpenMC agrees, with mild tension.** The 11 borated points differ by −409
  to +342 pcm with no trend (mean −53 pcm). Three of them sit at 2.0–2.4σ of a
  single run's σ. That is shown as it is. This is **verification**: two codes,
  not an experiment.
- *Caution:* this is $k_\infty$ of an infinite pin lattice, not the finite
  core, which leaks.

## Case study: test the assumption the result rests on

<div class="history">

**History, not the current result: the LCT-008 case differential (September
2026).**

On 2026-09-11 case 1 came out at **k = 1.02950 ± 0.00061, +2950 pcm** against a
measured critical experiment. The first explanation, argued from benchmark
coverage, blamed U-238 resonance escape. A prediction from another model
(+3200 pcm) even matched it to 8 %.

LCT-008 has several configurations, each critical on its own. They share one
pin cell and differ only in how the poison is supplied:

| case | soluble boron | poison rods | Δk (2026-09-11) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1511 ppm | none | +2950 ± 61 pcm |
| 2 | 1335.5 ppm | none | +2271 ± 61 pcm |
| 8 | 794 ppm | 144 pyrex | +1713 ± 60 pcm |

The pitch, pellet, clad and fuel are the same in all three, so an error in
resonance escape must give the **same** Δk in each. The spread was
**1237 ± 86 pcm, 14σ**. The benchmark's own other cases refuted the
explanation.

The real cause was found on 2026-09-14. A surface crossing was handed a
*global* position for a cylinder that lives inside a *shifted* lattice
element, so the normal was computed about the wrong centre (step 4's
question). A paired run at matched settings measured the fix at
**−2304 ± 267 pcm (8.6σ)** (commit `2655d83fa1`). The error scales with how
much rod surface a neutron crosses, which differs between the cases, and that
is what produced the spread.

A later note in the same record credited the collapse to a fix in the water's
scattering kernel instead. But both fixes had landed between the two runs it
compared, and the paired run isolates the geometry. The kernel's own share is
not resolved (−324 ± 227 pcm). That record was corrected on 2026-10-04
([`examples/lct008_keff.rs`](https://github.com/theodoreOnzGit/outram-park-backend/blob/@@COMMIT@@/crates/outram-mc-libs/examples/lct008_keff.rs)).

**Moral.** A prediction that matched to 8 % was a coincidence. The assumption
could be tested with data already in hand, and testing it is what found the
truth.

</div>

## 6. Is LCT-008 critical?

**The result to quote** comes from
[`five_route_keff_2026_09_29.md`](https://github.com/theodoreOnzGit/outram-park-backend/blob/@@COMMIT@@/crates/outram-mc-libs/verification_and_validation/icsbep/five_route_keff_2026_09_29.md),
section "Results — after the OpenMC-parity audit" (2026-09-30, `0414bc8277`).
It is **route 4, `outram-mc-libs` reading ENDF/B-VIII.0 directly**: 96 seeds,
each 10 000 neutrons × [250 + 400] generations:

$$k_\text{eff} = 1.00236 \pm 0.00008\quad(+236 \pm 8\ \text{pcm})$$

- **OpenMC on NJOY2016 data** (route 1, *which is OpenMC, not this code*)
  gives **1.00244 ± 0.00005 (+244 ± 5 pcm)**. The difference is −8 ± 9 pcm.
  The two codes agree with each other. Their shared offset of about 240 pcm
  from 1 therefore points at what they share, the evaluated data and the
  model, rather than at either code's transport.
- **What the ± means.** It is the standard error of the mean over 96
  independent seeds. A single run scatters by 74 pcm.
- **The reference is k = 1 by construction.** An ICSBEP critical experiment
  is reduced to a model whose k is 1. **No handbook uncertainty is quoted**,
  because the ICSBEP handbook is licence-restricted and is not in this
  project. So +236 pcm is a distance from 1, not a number of σ from the
  experiment. That makes it a **validation** against a measured experiment
  whose own uncertainty is not shown.
- **Deliberate liberty: 11 nuclides.** The record runs H-1, B-10, O-16,
  U-234/235/238, Al-27, Si-28/29/30 and Mn-55. It drops the clad's trace
  elements (Mg, Ti, Cr, Fe, Cu, Zn: 1.86 % of its atoms) and B-11, without
  renormalising, identically in both codes. ~~Their worth on the lattice has
  not been measured.~~ **Measured 2026-10-05**
  ([#533](https://github.com/theodoreOnzGit/outram-park-backend/issues/533),
  "Later measurements" in [the record](https://github.com/theodoreOnzGit/outram-park-backend/blob/@@COMMIT@@/crates/outram-mc-libs/verification_and_validation/icsbep/five_route_keff_2026_09_29.md), code `8b17079bc`). 16 runs of the
  full 35-nuclide lattice give +171 ± 19 pcm, against +236 ± 8 for 11
  nuclides. The dropped nuclides are therefore worth **−65 ± 21 pcm**
  (3.2σ), close to the −100 pcm predicted from their neutron capture. #533
  asked for 32 runs and remains open for the rest. The 11-nuclide number stays
  the one quoted, because OpenMC ran the same 11.
- **The one miss is shown:** outram-mc on NJOY2016's ACE files (route 3) sits
  −18 ± 8 pcm (2.3σ) from OpenMC.

**Run it yourself.**

