## What the transport loop actually calls

**Correction to the lesson plan in #524 (found 2026-10-04 while building this
walk):** the transport loop does **not** call `elastic_scatter` or
`two_body_scatter` for an elastic collision. In the scatter branch of
`transport_history_vr` it samples the centre-of-mass cosine itself, from the
nuclide's ENDF MF=4 law (`Nuclide::sample_elastic_mu_cm`) or, where the
evaluation has none, isotropically, `mu_cm = 2·prn − 1`, inline. It then calls
`free_gas_elastic_scatter_dbrc`, which **above 400 kT** (and outside a
nuclide's DBRC window) holds the target at rest and hands over to
`two_body_scatter_with_mu`, and that calls `cm_to_lab`, where α lives:

```text
E' = E (A² + 2 A μ_cm + 1) / (A + 1)²     so  μ_cm = −1 gives E' = α E,  μ_cm = +1 gives E' = E
```

## The textbook route: `elastic_scatter`

`elastic_scatter` is the isotropic-CM, target-at-rest form the lesson's
formula describes, and it is the oracle's kernel in
`examples/epithermal_slowing_down.rs` (section 1, where a nuclide has no
ENDF angular law). It is a chain of three one-line wrappers ending in the same
`cm_to_lab`, which is why the lesson can teach with it: the transport route
above differs only in where `μ_cm` comes from and in the free-gas gate. The
exercise "set `μ_cm = ±1`" is `two_body_scatter_with_mu(e, u, awr, 0.0, ±1.0, seed)`.
