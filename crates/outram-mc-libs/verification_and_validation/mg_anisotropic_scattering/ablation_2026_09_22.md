# MG anisotropic scattering — measured worth

GitHub **#265**. Measured 2026-09-22 by
`examples/mg_scatter_anisotropy_ablation.rs` at **128** paired seeds per arm,
and checked against **OpenMC's own multigroup mode** on the same library.

The prediction this is judged against was written **before** the program was
ever run, and is unedited since:
[`prediction_before_measuring.md`](prediction_before_measuring.md).

## Methodology

See the prediction file for the 2-group constants and the collapse. In short:
two arms over the same 32 seeds, the same geometry and the same macroscopic
set, differing **only** in the angular representation of the scattering matrix.

- **P3 arm** — every transfer carries `a = [1, 0.3, 0.1, 0.03]`, attached with
  `Mgxs::with_legendre_scattering`. The outgoing cosine is sampled from that
  kernel by rejection and the incoming direction rotated by it, mirroring
  `scatter` (`src/physics_mg.cpp:86-90`) at OpenMC `afa7a14`.
- **P0 arm** — the same set through `Mgxs::without_scatter_anisotropy()`: the
  outgoing direction is resampled isotropically in the lab, which is what this
  crate did for *every* MG set before this change.

Arms are paired (arm A and arm B share seed `s`); the quoted worth is the mean
of the 128 per-seed differences with the standard error of that mean.
128 paired seeds × 2000 histories × [30 inactive + 70 active] per arm
(17.9 M active histories per arm).

The reference side runs **OpenMC itself in multigroup mode** from
[`openmc_inputs/mg_p3_vs_p0_cube.py`](openmc_inputs/mg_p3_vs_p0_cube.py),
which builds the same constants and the same P3 moments through
`openmc.XSdata` / `openmc.MGXSLibrary` and exports them to HDF5. MG mode reads
no nuclear data, so this runs with **no `cross_sections.xml` and no ACE/ENDF
library present** — which is what makes a genuine code-to-code comparison
possible here at all.

## Results

### The ⟨μ⟩ each arm actually transports

| | group 0 | group 1 |
|---|---|---|
| P3 arm, `Mgxs::group_mean_cosine` | **0.300000** | **0.300000** |
| P0 arm, `Mgxs::group_mean_cosine` | **0.000000** | **0.000000** |

Exactly 0.3, not merely near it — the P3 kernel is positive on all of `[-1, 1]`
(shown in the prediction file), so there is no negativity truncation and the
sampled mean equals the declared moment. This is not automatic; see
`physics::scattdata` for the case where it is not.

### Bare cube, vacuum, a = 35 cm — the case under test

| arm | this crate (128 seeds) | OpenMC MG (100 k × 220 active) | Δ | predicted (1-group diffusion) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P3 (anisotropic) | **0.63356 ± 0.00028** | 0.63348 ± 0.00012 | **+8 ± 30 pcm (0.3 σ)** | 0.65409 |
| P0 (isotropic) | **0.67727 ± 0.00031** | 0.67771 ± 0.00012 | **−44 ± 33 pcm (1.3 σ)** | 0.69624 |
| **paired worth** | **−4371 ± 44 pcm (99.6 σ)** | −4423 ± 17 pcm | **+52 ± 47 pcm (1.1 σ)** | **−4200** |

*Superseded, kept because it shaped the reading:* a first pass at 32 seeds on
this side gave P3 `0.63383 ± 0.00057`, P0 `0.67691 ± 0.00070`, worth
`−4308 ± 93 pcm`. All three agree with the 128-seed values within statistics.

### Control: reflective cube, a = 10 cm — zero leakage, must not move

| arm | this crate (128 seeds) | OpenMC MG (20 k × 70 active) | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| P3 (anisotropic) | **1.09977 ± 0.00030** | 1.09987 ± 0.00014 | −10 ± 33 pcm (0.3 σ) |
| P0 (isotropic) | **1.09978 ± 0.00032** | 1.09976 ± 0.00013 | +2 ± 35 pcm (0.1 σ) |
| **paired worth** | **−1 ± 38 pcm (0.0 σ)** | +11 ± 20 pcm | — |

Both codes, in both arms, reproduce the set's closed-form `k∞ = 1.10000`.

## Interpretation

**The prediction held on all four points it committed to.**

1. **Sign negative** — predicted, measured. Forward-peaked scattering lengthens
   the transport mean free path and so raises leakage out of a finite system.
   Same sign, same mechanism as `op-tm9f` on the continuous-energy side.
2. **Magnitude −4371 ± 44 pcm against −4200 predicted** — 4.1 % out, deep
   inside the stated ±30 % band, and the prediction was made from one-group
   diffusion on constants collapsed from the infinite-medium spectrum with no
   reference to any run.
3. **The control is consistent with zero** at 0.0 σ (−1 ± 38 pcm; OpenMC's own
   control is +11 ± 20). This is the load-bearing
   check, not a formality: it is what says the bare-cube move is a leakage
   effect rather than two errors that now cancel. `CLAUDE.md` records the same
   control being what made `op-tm9f` believable.
4. **`group_mean_cosine` is exactly 0.3 against exactly 0.**

**Against OpenMC's own MG mode, every comparison agrees within 1.3 σ** —
`+8 ± 30` pcm on the anisotropic arm, `−44 ± 33` on the isotropic one,
`+52 ± 47` on the worth itself, and both arms of the reflective control inside
0.3 σ. That is a code-to-code check of the sampling, not only of the physics
argument: the two codes draw the outgoing cosine by the same rejection scheme
but through independent implementations and independent RNG streams.

**A near-miss on the reference side, recorded because it nearly became a
finding.** The *first* OpenMC run used 20 000 particles × 70 active batches and
gave bare-P3 `0.63222 ± 0.00054`, which sits **2.1 σ** from this crate's value
and would have read as a real discrepancy in the anisotropic arm specifically
(the isotropic arm agreed to 0.4 σ, which is exactly the pattern a genuine
anisotropy bug would produce). Re-running the *reference* at 100 000 × 220
moved it to `0.63348 ± 0.00012` — **2.3 σ from its own earlier draw** — and the
disagreement evaporated. The lesson is the one `CLAUDE.md` already records in
another form: *a reference is not right merely for being external*, and a
2 σ gap against an under-converged reference is not evidence about our code.
Both reference runs are reported here; neither was discarded for being
inconvenient.

**A qualification the number should not be read past.** The two *absolute*
predictions are 2.8 % and 3.1 % high (diffusion theory on a 70 cm cube with a
~15 cm extrapolation distance is not exact, and was not claimed to be), yet the
*difference* is predicted to 2.6 %. That is not luck — the diffusion error is
almost common-mode between arms and cancels in the pairing. It also means this
study prices the **anisotropy**, and says nothing about how well the MG kernel
reproduces an absolute eigenvalue; the reflective-cube control, which does hit
its closed form, is the only absolute check here.

**What it does not cover.** One set, one anisotropy (`⟨μ⟩ = 0.3`, uniform over
all four transfers), two geometries, no upscatter, and a kernel deliberately
chosen not to go negative. A real MG library has a per-transfer `⟨μ⟩` that
varies across the matrix and moments that *do* go negative, where the sampled
`⟨μ⟩` is strictly smaller than the declared one — quantified in
`physics::scattdata` but not exercised on an eigenvalue here.
