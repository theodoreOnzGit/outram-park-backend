# Survival biasing — measured worth, and a residual resolved to a bound

GitHub **#258** scope items 1 and 2. Measured 2026-09-22 by
`examples/variance_reduction_ablation.rs`.

The predictions this is judged against were written **before** the runs and
are unedited:
[`prediction_before_measuring.md`](prediction_before_measuring.md).

## Methodology

Bare HEU sphere at Godiva's critical radius (8.7407 cm), U-235 + U-238 from
`reference-data/endf/` at 293.6 K, `[15 inactive + 40 active]` generations,
single-thread CPU backend. Two arms over the same seeds, differing **only** in
`KeffSettings::variance_reduction`:

- **ANALOG** — the default; capture kills the particle.
- **SURVIVAL** — `survival_biasing: true` with upstream's defaults
  (`weight_cutoff = 0.25`, `weight_survive = 1.0`).

Reported per arm: the ensemble mean of `k` with its standard error, the
seed-to-seed standard deviation, the wall-clock, and
`FOM = 1 / (sd² · t)`.

## Results

| `N` | seeds | analog `k` | survival `k` | **survival − analog** | sd ratio | cost ratio | FOM ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2000 | 24 | 0.99236 ± 0.00103 | 0.99448 ± 0.00079 | **+212 ± 130 pcm (1.63 σ)** | 1.31× | 1.44× | 1.18× |
| 2000 | 96 | 0.99300 ± 0.00052 | 0.99422 ± 0.00034 | **+122 ± 62 pcm (1.97 σ)** | 1.49× | 1.45× | 1.55× |
| 8000 | 32 | 0.99218 ± 0.00052 | 0.99300 ± 0.00036 | **+83 ± 64 pcm (1.30 σ)** | 1.46× | 1.45× | 1.47× |
| 32000 | 48 | 0.99249 ± 0.00018 | 0.99263 ± 0.00013 | **+14 ± 22 pcm (0.61 σ)** | 1.41× | 1.44× | 1.38× |

The seeds are `20260922 + s·7919` for `s ∈ 0..n`, so **the 24-seed row's seeds
are a strict subset of the 96-seed row's**. That row is superseded, not
confirmatory, and it is excluded from every fit below. It is kept in the table
because it is what was run first and because its 1.63 σ is part of why the
early reading looked like a bias.

## What held, and what did not

**Three of the four predictions held and tightened rather than moved.**

| | predicted | measured (all four rows) |
|---|---|---|
| sd ratio | 1.2–1.5× | **1.31–1.49×** |
| FOM ratio | 0.5–1.5× (i.e. *predicted not to pay*) | **1.18–1.55×** |
| cost ratio | 1.5–3× | **1.44–1.45×** |

The cost ratio came in **below** the predicted floor, so that part of the
prediction was wrong and is recorded as such. It is wrong *consistently* —
1.44, 1.45, 1.45, 1.44 across four runs spanning a factor of 16 in `N` — so
this is a mis-estimate of the technique's cost, not a noisy miss. The FOM landed just above the
predicted band: survival biasing does pay on this problem, by about 1.5×,
where the prediction said it might not pay at all. That is the prediction
being too pessimistic rather than the measurement being surprising — the cost
was overestimated, and the FOM is the ratio of the two.

**The fourth prediction — unbiasedness — WAS not resolved by the first three
rows, and is now resolved to a bound by the fourth.** The history is kept
here rather than rewritten, because the reason the first attempt failed is
the useful part.

Prediction 1 said the difference should be **zero**. Across the first three
rows it was not resolved either way, and the significance went *up*, not down,
as statistics improved (1.63 σ → 1.97 σ → 1.30 σ). Worse, the `N = 2000` and
`N = 8000` values differed by `39 ± 89 pcm` — **statistically
indistinguishable**. The central value fell by a factor 1.47 where
population-control bias predicts 4, but those error bars could not tell a
factor of 4 from a factor of 1. **That test was underpowered**, and calling it
"consistent with population control" would have been exactly the failure this
crate's own history records — the hybrid-tracking gate that was "inside 4 σ
and equally consistent with a real 1200 pcm bias".

The `N = 32000` row settles it as far as it can be settled: **+14 ± 22 pcm**,
0.61 σ from zero, with the asymptotic bias bounded at **|b| ≤ 57 pcm at 2 σ**
by the fit in the next section. Survival biasing is unbiased on this problem
to within 57 pcm, and the only honest reason that bound is not tighter is that
nothing here has been run long enough to make it so.

## The hypothesis, and what the discriminating run decided

**Fission-bank population control.** Power iteration with a fixed-size bank
carries an eigenvalue bias of order `1/N` whose coefficient is proportional to
the variance of the per-history fission production. Both arms carry it.
Survival biasing reduces that variance by construction — that is what it is
for — so the two arms sit at slightly different points of the same `1/N` curve
at finite `N`. This is a property of power iteration, not a defect in the
estimator, and its **sign is right**: the lower-variance arm has the smaller
bias, so `survival − analog > 0`, which is what is measured.

Addendum 1 of the prediction file pre-registered the discriminating run, with
both outcomes written down before it was launched:

| hypothesis | predicted at `N = 32000` | measured |
|---|---|---|
| population control (`1/N` from `N = 2000`) | ≈ **+8 pcm** | |
| a real estimator bias | ≈ **+83 to +122 pcm** | |
| **measured, 48 seeds** | | **+14 ± 22 pcm (0.61 σ)** |

**The residual did not stay near +83 pcm**, so the pre-registered conclusion
that "survival biasing in this port has a defect that must be found" is **not
triggered**. The `1/N` prediction of +8 pcm sits 0.3 σ from the measurement.

### How strong that is, stated exactly

The temptation here is to write "population control confirmed" and stop. The
arithmetic does not support that wording, and the earlier rows' error bars are
why.

Fitting all three independent rows to `r(N) = b + c/N` — weighted least
squares, the `N = 2000`/24-seed row **excluded** because its seeds are a strict
subset of the 96-seed row (`seed = 20260922 + s·7919`, `s ∈ 0..n`) and it is
therefore superseded rather than independent:

| parameter | fitted | reading |
|---|---|---|
| `b` (asymptotic bias) | **+11 ± 23 pcm** | 0.45 σ from zero |
| `c` (the `1/N` term) | (2.4 ± 1.4)·10⁵ pcm·histories | **1.71 σ** from zero |
| χ² | 0.49 on 1 dof | the two-parameter model fits |

A constant-bias-only model gives `b = +31.5 ± 19.7 pcm` with χ² = 3.41 on
2 dof. The improvement from adding the `1/N` term is Δχ² = 2.9, i.e. about
**1.7 σ**.

So, precisely:

- **What is established:** survival biasing shows **no detectable bias** at
  `N = 32000`, and the asymptotic bias is bounded at **|b| ≤ 57 pcm at 2 σ**.
- **What is favoured but NOT established:** that the finite-`N` residual is
  population control. It is preferred over a constant bias at only 1.7 σ.
  Consistency is not confirmation, and this row is the one a later reader is
  most likely to over-read.
- **What the run did rule out:** the run was pre-registered as ~4 σ
  discriminating, and against the *point* values +83 and +122 it is 3.1 σ and
  4.9 σ. But those were themselves measurements carrying ±64 and ±62, and
  tested against the measurements rather than their central values the
  separation is only **1.0 σ and 1.6 σ**. The pre-registration overstated the
  power of its own test by treating a noisy central value as exact. The 2 σ
  bound above is the defensible statement; "+83 excluded at 3 σ" is not.

**One correlation is not quantified.** All three rows draw from the same seed
sequence, so they are not strictly independent. Different `N` means completely
different history streams, so the correlation is expected to be small, but it
has not been measured and the quoted uncertainties do not include it.

## The property that is not in doubt

Whatever the residual turns out to be, it belongs to the **survival-biased**
arm. The analog path is untouched: `the_analog_path_is_bit_identical_to_the_pre_258_build`
asserts `k_mean = 9.91850110380556260e-1` as an exact `f64` equality against a
value measured on the **parent commit** `9b861a861`, before any of #258
existed. Every recorded V&V number in this crate was measured through that
path, and a single extra RNG draw would have made all of them unreproducible
while nothing else in the suite noticed.
