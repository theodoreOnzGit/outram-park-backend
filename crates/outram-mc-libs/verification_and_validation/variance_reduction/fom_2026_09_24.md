# Weight-window figure of merit on the shielded room — measured

**Date:** 2026-09-24
**Issue:** [gh:#258](https://github.com/theodoreOnzGit/outram-park-backend/issues/258),
acceptance bullet 3: *"A figure of merit (FOM = 1/(sigma^2 \* t)) improvement
reported for the shielding case, measured rather than asserted."*
**Test:** `crates/outram-mc-libs/tests/openmc_notebooks/shielded_room_weight_window.rs`
(live, `long-tests`, ~9 min).
**Supersedes:** every FOM figure previously reported on #258. They were computed
through a `rel_std_dev` that was `sqrt(n)` too large (see "The defect that had to
be fixed first").

## Headline

**Weight windows do NOT improve the figure of merit on this configuration in the
region where the analog arm can also resolve — except in the far field, where
they cross into genuine benefit. Their real contribution is 43 mesh cells the
analog arm cannot resolve at all, where a FOM ratio does not exist.**

That is the honestly-derived answer, and it is a miss against the hoped-for one.

## Methodology

Two arms on the same geometry, materials, source and mesh (31 x 38 cells,
50 cm), 8 nuclides reconstructed from ENDF/B-VIII.0 at 293.6 K:

| arm | particles | realizations | wall clock |
|---|---|---|---|
| analog | 50 000 | 10 (5 000 each) | 136.3 s |
| weight windows | 26 000 | 260 (100 each) | 405.2 s transport + 9.2 s generation |

`FOM = 1/(R^2 t)` per mesh cell, with `R` the bin's relative standard error and
`t` that arm's own wall clock.

**The arms are deliberately NOT matched in cost, and that is the point.**
`FOM = 1/(R^2 t)` is cost-normalised by construction: `R^2 ~ 1/N` and `t ~ N`, so
the quantity is invariant in run length and two arms at different costs are
exactly what it compares. An earlier version of this test additionally forced
matched wall-clock — a constraint the acceptance criterion never asked for — and
that is why the criterion read **NOT MEASURABLE** for two days. See "Three
attempts at bounding the window arm" below.

Cells are binned by penetration depth rather than reported as one number, because
a single figure cannot say where windows pay and where they do not — which is the
entire content of the result.

A cell qualifies for a FOM only at `R <= 0.5`, fixed before any figure of merit
was computed. At 10 realizations that admits a cell resolved in roughly 4 batches
of 10 or better and rejects the one- and two-hit cells whose variance estimate is
dominated by its own sampling noise. Deep penetration is exactly where two lucky
scores would otherwise manufacture a flattering ratio.

### The interpretive line that makes the numbers checkable

Per-particle cost: analog **2.73 ms**, windows **15.58 ms** — a cost ratio of
**5.72x**. So:

> A FOM ratio of **1 / 5.72 = 0.175x** means the windows bought **zero variance
> benefit** in that cell and charged their splitting overhead for nothing.
> Above 0.175x is real benefit; below it is active harm.

Without that line a ratio of 0.15 is an arbitrary number. With it, the ratios
decompose into cost and benefit and can be reasoned about.

## Results

| band | FOM ratio (transport only) | including generation | cells | vs 0.175 line | windows-only cells |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–400 cm (source side) | 0.153x | 0.149x | 105 | slight **harm** | 5 |
| 400–900 cm (mid-field) | 0.117x | 0.114x | 289 | **harm** | 10 |
| 900–1450 cm (far field) | **0.252x** | 0.246x | 241 | **benefit** (1.44x variance) | 28 |
| beyond 1450 cm (deep) | not measurable | — | 0 | — | 0 |

**43 cells in total were resolved by the window arm and not by the analog arm.**
Their FOM ratio is **undefined** — the analog FOM does not exist there — not
infinite, and the count is the result. Reporting those as an infinite ratio, or
folding them into the geometric means, is how a shielding FOM gets overstated;
they are kept in a separate column.

Aggregation is by **geometric** mean over cells. An arithmetic mean of ratios is
dominated by the single best cell, and the far-field range runs to 3.433x.

### What the numbers say

1. **Where the analog arm is already adequate, windows cost and do not pay.**
   0.153x and 0.117x are at or below the zero-benefit line: splitting overhead
   with no variance return, which is textbook behaviour for a well-sampled
   region and not a defect.
2. **The ratio improves with penetration depth and crosses into benefit in the
   far field.** 0.252x against a 0.175x break-even is a real 1.44x variance
   reduction, partially offsetting the 5.72x cost.
3. **The benefit that matters is coverage, and FOM cannot express it.** 43 cells
   have an answer from the window arm and none from the analog arm. For a
   shielding calculation that is the whole point, and it is invisible to a ratio.
4. **Charging generation barely matters here** (0.153 -> 0.149): MAGIC is 9.2 s
   against 405 s of transport. It is reported both ways anyway, because which
   cost to charge is a judgement about the question being asked and hiding the
   choice is how the number gets inflated.

### Unbiasedness — the gate that can actually fail

Weight windows are an estimator, not physics: they may cost anything, but they
must not move the answer. Flux per source particle: analog **1712.43**, windows
**1750.27**, **+2.21 %**, inside the 25 % bound. This is the assertion in the
test; the FOM is reported and never gated, because a threshold on a figure of
merit would be a number chosen after seeing it.

## The defect that had to be fixed first

`TallyBin::rel_std_dev` returns the relative standard error **of the mean**. Its
two branches computed different quantities:

| branch | computes | |
|---|---|---|
| `sum_sq` path | `(sum_sq/n - mean^2)/(n-1)` = `s^2/n` exactly | correct |
| Welford path | `variance()` = `s^2` | **`sqrt(n)` too large** |

`variance()` is the sample variance of the realization *values*; the extra `/n`
is what makes it the variance of their *mean*, and it was missing from the
Welford path from the day that path was added. Verified independently on
`[1,2,3,4,5]`: true `R = 0.235702`, the Welford path gave `0.527046`, ratio
exactly `sqrt(5)`.

**It surfaced because more statistics made the reported error worse.** This
tally resolved 384 cells at 10 realizations and **2 at 270**, since `R` was
inflated by `sqrt(270) = 16.4` against `sqrt(10) = 3.2`. No correct estimator
behaves that way, and that is what pointed at the function rather than at the
configuration.

**The test guarding it pinned the regression.**
`a_well_conditioned_bin_is_unchanged` — written to confirm that adding Welford
"did not move ordinary results" — asserted `sqrt(2.5)/3`, the sample standard
deviation over the mean, where the standard error of the mean is `sqrt(2.5/5)/3`.
It now asserts the SEM *and* that the two branches agree, which is the check that
would have caught it: two formulas for one quantity must return one number.

**The doc was right and the code was wrong.** This test's own comment tabulates
`R = sqrt((n-k)/(k(n-1)))` as 1.00/0.67/0.51/0.41/0.33 for `k = 1..5` at
`n = 10`. Recomputed, that closed form **is** the corrected value to four
decimals — the comment described the right statistics and the implementation did
not match it. The table became true with the fix rather than needing one.

**Blast radius, checked rather than assumed.** MAGIC is **not** affected:
`update_magic` computes `(sum_sq/nf - mean^2)/(nf-1)` itself, so no window was
ever granted or refused on an inflated error, and the frontier numbers in
`magic_frontier_2026_09_23.md` stand. Checked first, because had MAGIC been
affected every one of those would have been suspect. All other callers re-run
green: `--lib` 406 pass, and `tally_power_normalization` / `flux_spectrum` /
`expansion_filters` 1 each — their gates were loose enough that a 3x inflated
error never tripped them, which is why it sat undetected.

## Three attempts at bounding the window arm, and one error class

The arm has been bounded wrongly three times, always the same way: **a bound
placed outside the unit of work cannot bound the unit of work.**

1. **Matched wall-clock with adaptive chunks.** Starved the arm to 7 690
   particles *and* delivered them as **1 200 realizations of ~6 particles**, so
   `R` sat near 1 across the mesh and nothing qualified at all. The FOM read NOT
   MEASURABLE, and the cause was the driver, not the windows.
2. **Fixed particle count, time asserted afterwards.** An assertion after the run
   cannot bound the run: at 751 windows the arm ran **2165 s without finishing
   10 000 particles**.
3. **Clock checked between chunks.** Only as fine as one chunk — and at 751
   windows one 1000-particle chunk exceeded the whole budget.

The current design: a **fixed** 1000-particle chunk (100 per realization — few,
but real realizations rather than 6-particle ones), the clock checked between
chunks, and a preflight probe that **refuses rather than sizes**. That last
distinction is the whole point: sizing from a probe ran for hours twice, because
probe particles start near the source where windows are dense and histories die
fast, so a probe always *underestimates* a steered particle. But "the probe alone
already blew the budget" is a valid non-extrapolated conclusion, and refusing on
it costs at most the probe.

## A cost pathology, measured and unexplained

| MAGIC iterations | windows (of 1178) | cost per steered particle |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 621 | **15.6 ms** |
| 6 | 751 | **> 1.56 s** |

A **21 % increase in window count for a ~100x increase in cost.** Both
6-iteration figures are *lower* bounds — two runs were killed by timeout, so the
true cost is unknown.

This is not a smooth cost curve and **no cause is asserted**. Leading hypothesis:
MAGIC sets a cell's lower bound from the flux estimate there; deep cells have
very little flux, hence very low bounds; a particle arriving with weight far
above the upper bound splits, and each daughter one cell deeper splits again.
`max_split` caps splits **per event**, which this crate enforces and tests, but
nothing caps the cascade **along a path**. A window set built from a starved
deep-flux estimate would look exactly like this. It is recorded as a hypothesis
with a mechanism, not as a finding.

Consequence: the test's default is **1** MAGIC iteration, the set it can afford,
with that table in the source so the price of exploring further is stated in
advance. 621 of 1178 cells carrying a window is a legitimate window set; what it
is not is the deepest MAGIC can build.

## Limitations

1. **One configuration.** One geometry, one mesh resolution (50 cm), one source,
   one window set (621 of 1178 cells, 1 MAGIC iteration). Nothing here says what
   windows are worth on a better-converged set — and the cost pathology above is
   why a better-converged set was not measured.
2. **The notebook's deep-penetration claim is still not reproduced.** The window
   arm reached **1 of 68** cells beyond 200 cm of concrete; the analog arm
   reached 0. The frontier study (`magic_frontier_2026_09_23.md`) measured it
   advancing at ~23 cells per MAGIC iteration, needing roughly 20 more
   iterations — which the cost pathology now makes far more expensive than that
   estimate assumed.
3. **Single seed per arm.** No seed ensemble, so the per-cell ratios carry
   sampling noise beyond the `R <= 0.5` admission cut. The band geometric means
   over 105–289 cells are more robust than any single cell, and the ranges are
   reported for that reason.
4. **`t` includes ENDF reconstruction for neither arm** — it is paid once before
   both and is not part of either arm's transport time.
