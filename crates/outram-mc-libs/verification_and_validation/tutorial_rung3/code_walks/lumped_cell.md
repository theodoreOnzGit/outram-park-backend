## 2. and 3. The flight meets a surface

In `transport_history_vr` the flight length is the shorter of the collision
distance and `geom.distance_to_boundary(&path)`; when the boundary wins, the
neutron is moved to the surface and `geom.cross_surface_in_frame(..)` decides
what happens. These are **trait methods** (`GeometryExt`, implemented for
`Geometry` in `src/geometry/crossing/mod.rs`, because the CSG types live in
`outram-blender` since #486). rust-analyzer resolves them to the
implementation, so the walk below is generated, not filled by hand; what the
two crossings *mean* is:

- crossing the **lump surface** (transmissive): the neutron is relocated into
  the cell on the other side, and the next flight uses the other material's
  `Σ_t`. That is all spatial self-shielding is in the code: at a U-238 peak
  `Σ_t` in the lump is so large that the flight ends within microns of the
  surface. **There is no self-shielding function.**
- crossing the **cell surface** (`BoundaryType::White`): `cross_surface`
  calls `SurfaceKindExt::diffuse_reflect`, a port of OpenMC's
  `Surface::diffuse_reflect` (`src/surface.cpp:144`): keep the crossing point,
  send the neutron back in with a cosine-law direction about the normal. For
  a sphere that is the Wigner-Seitz white condition. A *specular* sphere
  (`BoundaryType::Reflective`) would keep each neutron's impact parameter for
  ever, which is the trap `lump_self_shielding_scan.rs`'s `vv_gate` records.
