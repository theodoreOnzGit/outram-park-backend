**Modules:** `errorr` (multigroup covariances), `covr` (formats them), and the

## The question

Every cross section on this site was printed without an error bar, yet the
evaluator fitted it to measurements that had one. **How uncertain is σ, and
are the uncertainties at different energies independent?**

## The shortest answer

Modern evaluations carry **covariance files**: MF=33 for cross sections,
MF=32 for resonance parameters, MF=31 for ν̄, MF=34 for angular and MF=35 for
energy distributions. They are almost never independent from energy to
energy: if a normalisation measurement was 1 % high, every energy it touched
moves together. ERRORR collapses those covariances to a group structure, the
same way GROUPR collapses cross sections; COVR prints them as relative
covariances or correlations (the BOXER library format) for sensitivity and
uncertainty codes.

## The formulas

The group-collapsed covariance of reactions `x` and `y` in groups `g` and `h`,
from a pointwise relative covariance `C_xy(E, E')` and the weighting flux
`φ`:

```text
cov(σ_x,g , σ_y,h) = ∫_g ∫_h  σ_x(E) σ_y(E') C_xy(E, E') φ(E) φ(E') dE dE'
                     ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                                 ∫_g φ(E) dE  ·  ∫_h φ(E') dE'
```

MF=33's "NI sub-subsections" (`LB = 0…8`) are different shorthand forms of
`C_xy`, from fully correlated within a range (`LB = 0, 1`) to full matrices
(`LB = 5`).

For resonance parameters `p` with covariance `C_p` (MF=32), the cross-section
covariance follows by **first-order propagation** ("the sandwich rule"):

```text
C_σ = S · C_p · Sᵀ,        S_gi = ∂σ_g / ∂p_i
```

which needs the derivative of every group cross section with respect to every
resonance parameter: for R-matrix-limited evaluations, SAMMY's derivative
machinery (`samm::derivs`).

**Not ported, stated** (the porting plan's list): `LCOMP = 0` (no evaluation
here has it, #171), `LRF = 1` ERRORJ sensitivities, `NRO ≠ 0`, `NLRS > 0`,
MF=31/34/35/40, GENDF input (`colaps`), and COVR's PostScript plotting. A
lesson on ν̄ or angular-distribution uncertainties cannot be written from this
crate yet; those rows are visible in the [coverage table](./coverage.md).

**Verification, not validation**, and with a special caveat: a covariance is
itself an evaluator's judgement. Reproducing NJOY's collapse of it says the
arithmetic is right, nothing about whether the uncertainty is.

## Predict

We now have σ(E) at temperature, unresolved tables, thermal laws, heating,
groups and covariances, all in memory. A Monte Carlo code written in another
language (OpenMC, MCNP, Serpent) cannot read Rust memory. **What would a file
have to contain for a transport code to run without ever seeing the ENDF
tape? Just σ(E)?**
