## The question

[Rung 1](./endf.md) ended with 3 345 resonances on U-238's tape and no curve.
**How do a few numbers per resonance become σ(E) at every energy a transport
code might ask for?**

## The shortest answer

Each resonance formalism is a formula that gives σ(E) at any one energy from
the parameters. RECONR (NJOY's manual, [MacFarlane et al., 2017](#ref-njoy2016): it *"reconstructs pointwise
(energy-dependent) cross sections from ENDF resonance parameters and
interpolation schemes"*, §1.1, p. 1) evaluates that formula on an energy grid
and **adds points until straight lines between them reproduce the formula to a
tolerance**, typically 0.1 %. The result is a PENDF ("pointwise ENDF"): MF=3
tables, linear-linear, on one union grid shared by every reaction.

## The formula

The simplest formalism, single-level Breit–Wigner (SLBW, `LRF = 1`), for one
resonance at `E_r` with neutron, capture and fission widths `Γ_n, Γ_γ, Γ_f`:

```text
σ_γ(E) = (π/k²) g_J  Γ_n(E) Γ_γ / [ (E − E_r)² + (Γ/2)² ]
σ_f(E) = (π/k²) g_J  Γ_n(E) Γ_f / [ (E − E_r)² + (Γ/2)² ]

  k      neutron wave number in the centre-of-mass frame, ∝ √E
  g_J    = (2J+1) / (2(2I+1)), the spin statistical factor
  Γ_n(E) = Γ_n · P_l(E) / P_l(E_r), the neutron width scaled by the penetrability
  Γ      = Γ_n(E) + Γ_γ + Γ_f
```

Elastic scattering adds a potential-scattering term and an interference term
with it; the full set is the module doc of
[`reconr/slbw.rs`](https://github.com/theodoreOnzGit/outram-park-backend/blob/@@COMMIT@@/crates/njoy-outram-park-fork/src/reconr/slbw.rs).
The other formalisms refine this picture:

| `LRF` | formalism | what it adds | port |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | SLBW | one level at a time; no interference between levels | `reconr/slbw.rs` |
| 2 | MLBW | level–level interference in elastic | `reconr/slbw.rs` |
| 3 | Reich–Moore | an R-matrix with eliminated capture channels; the modern default (U-235, U-238) | `reconr/rm.rs` |
| 4 | Adler–Adler | an older empirical multi-reaction fit | `reconr/aa.rs` |
| 7 | R-matrix limited | the full multichannel R-matrix, any number of particle pairs | `samm/` + `reconr/rml.rs` |

The issue that set up this track said "the four formalisms"; the port carries
**five** (Adler–Adler included), and all five reach `reconr()`.

## Why a grid, and how dense

Between two resonances σ(E) is smooth; across one it rises and falls by orders
of magnitude within a few widths. RECONR's adaptive rule (`resxs` upstream):
take a panel `[E_1, E_2]`, evaluate the exact formula at the midpoint, and
compare with the straight line:

```text
accept the panel if   | σ_exact(E_mid) − σ_linear(E_mid) |  ≤  err · σ_exact(E_mid)
otherwise split it at E_mid and test both halves
```

On top of the resonance nodes (`E_r ± Γ/2`, rounded to the tape's significant
figures) the port reproduces upstream's `lunion` decade points and step cap,
and `emerge`'s merge of every reaction onto **one** grid.

## Case study: Fe-57 and one wrong operand (GitHub #339)

**The symptom.** Building ENDF/B-VIII.0 [(Brown & others, 2018)](#ref-brown2018endf8) **Fe-57** (in the Al-6061 cladding of
the LCT-008 lattice) exhausted memory: `memory allocation of 1476395008 bytes
failed` under a 14 GB cap. A profile showed 71 % of CPU in the `LRF = 7` path
and a third of all CPU in `malloc`, which looked like a performance problem.

**What upstream said.** Fe-57's MF=2 is `LRF = 7` with a third particle pair,
inelastic scattering to the 14.4 keV level, and two spin groups with **four**
coupled channels. Inverting a four-channel level matrix goes through SAMMY's
LINPACK-style `xspsl`. Reading `samm.f90:6189, 6201-6204` showed that the
second operand of `xdot` is the **right-hand side `b`**. The port read it from
the packed factor:

```rust,ignore
{{#include ../../../src/samm/linpack.rs:lesson_xdot}}
```

With the wrong operand, `|Y·Y⁻¹ − I|` reached 40 and the channel unitarity sum
`Σ|U|²` reached 9 120 (its bound is 1). The inelastic channel came out up to
10⁴ times too large, so capture, computed as non-elastic minus inelastic, went
**negative** (down to −4.2 × 10⁴ b).

**Why a wrong number became an out-of-memory.** RECONR's midpoint test can
never pass on a negative cross section, so every panel was bisected down to the
significant-figure floor. Upstream guards against this
(`reconr.f90:2641-2645`, `if (sigp(j).lt.zero) sigp(j)=0`); the port had not
carried the guard:

```rust,ignore
{{#include ../../../src/reconr/mod.rs:lesson_clamp}}
```

**The ablation recorded with the fix** (test doc of
[`tests/reconr_lrf7_threshold_channels_vs_njoy2016.rs`](https://github.com/theodoreOnzGit/outram-park-backend/blob/@@COMMIT@@/crates/njoy-outram-park-fork/tests/reconr_lrf7_threshold_channels_vs_njoy2016.rs),
2026-10-01, fix commit `4171473f48`):

| variant | result on Fe-57 |
|---|---|
| neither fix | out of memory after ~2.5 min |
| clamp only | bounded (0.15 s, 26 MB) but **wrong**: 24 545 points against NJOY's 17 213, 285 212 differing words |
| `xdot` only | identical to NJOY (no partial goes negative) |
| both | identical to NJOY: 82 sections, 17 213 points, **0** differing words, 0.10 s, 24 MB |

**The lesson.** A wrong matrix inverse looked like a slow kernel. The
per-point allocation in the profile was a symptom; reading the upstream routine
that owns the operation found the cause in one line. The clamp alone would have
"fixed" the crash and shipped a wrong cross section.
