## The question

Every rung so far treated the target as a free atom, either at rest or moving
in a Maxwellian gas. A 0.025 eV neutron has a wavelength of about 1.8 Å, the
same as the spacing of carbon atoms in graphite. **Does the neutron still see a
free carbon atom?**

## The shortest answer

No. At thermal energies the neutron scatters off the **whole lattice or
molecule**: it can give energy to, or take energy from, phonons and molecular
vibrations, and in a crystal it can diffract (Bragg scattering). All of that
is packed into one function per material, the **thermal scattering law**
`S(α, β)`, stored in ENDF **MF=7**. THERMR turns it into cross sections and
outgoing energy–angle distributions; LEAPR generates `S(α, β)` from a physical
model of the material's vibrations. The manual's one-line summaries
(§1.1, pp. 1–2): THERMR *"produces cross sections and energy-to-energy
matrices for free or bound scatterers in the thermal energy range"*; LEAPR
*"produces thermal scattering data in ENDF-6 File 7 format that can be
processed using the THERMR module"*.

## The formula

Incoherent inelastic scattering, from incident energy `E` to `E'` through
cosine `μ`, per bound atom of cross section `σ_b` at temperature `T`:

```text
d²σ/dE' dμ (E → E', μ) = (σ_b / 2kT) · √(E'/E) · e^(−β/2) · S(α, β)

  α = ( E' + E − 2 μ √(E E') ) / (A k T)     momentum transfer, dimensionless
  β = ( E' − E ) / (k T)                        energy transfer, dimensionless
```

(This is the symmetric form of `S` that ENDF tabulates, so the detailed-balance
factor `e^(−β/2)` appears explicitly.) Two elastic channels sit beside it:
**coherent elastic** (Bragg edges, crystalline graphite, σ(E) = S(E,T)/E) and
**incoherent elastic** (hydrogen in ZrH).

LEAPR builds `S` in the incoherent Gaussian approximation from a **phonon
frequency spectrum** ρ(ω), by the phonon expansion

```text
S(α, β) = e^(−α λ) · Σ_n  (α λ)ⁿ / n!  · T_n(β)

  λ     the Debye–Waller coefficient, from ρ(ω)
  T_n   the n-phonon term: T_1 from ρ(ω), T_n = T_1 convolved with T_(n−1)
```

plus optional translational (free-gas or diffusion) and discrete-oscillator
terms (water's bending and stretching modes).

## Where the Monte Carlo code switches

The transport kernel in `outram-mc-libs` picks its scattering model by energy:
S(α,β) below the evaluation's own `E_max` (the cutoff stored with MF=7, since
#459), free gas below about 400 kT, and a target at rest above. This crate
supplies the S(α,β) branch through
[`thermr::scattering`](https://github.com/theodoreOnzGit/outram-park-backend/blob/@@COMMIT@@/crates/njoy-outram-park-fork/src/thermr/scattering.rs):
`IncoherentInelasticScattering`, `CoherentElasticScattering`,
`IncoherentElasticScattering`, each resolved at a temperature at construction.

**Temperatures are resolved, never snapped.** A requested temperature that
matches a tabulated one within NJOY's tolerance uses it; otherwise the ENDF
`LI` interpolation law is applied between tabulated temperatures; outside the
range the request is refused with `TemperatureOutOfRange`.

**A correction this page had to make.** The crate's `CLAUDE.md` listed the
graphite check as "every stored MT=4 `S` is bit-identical". The example itself
had corrected that on 2026-09-11: 6 645 of 60 000 stored values are
bit-identical, and the real result is the 1e-13 agreement above. The table row
now carries the strike-through.

**A defect class worth naming: the constants depend on the evaluation's
date.** ENDF/B-VIII.0 graphite's deck says `EVAL-SEP17`, which selects NJOY's
pre-2018 physical constants (Boltzmann's constant among them). Using CODATA
2018 instead costs about a factor of 100 on the inelastic channel and 1e7 on
the elastic one; `leapr::vintage` selects the set from the deck's own card.

**Not covered, stated:** multi-scatterer mixing (`nmix > 1`), the cold
hydrogen/deuterium (`coldh`) path beyond self-consistency, and a THERMR
card-deck driver (`thermr::run()` is `NotPorted`; the typed API is the entry).
Whether S(α,β) models describe real graphite or water is **validation**, and it
belongs to the thermal benchmarks (HST-009, LCT-008) in the Monte Carlo track.
