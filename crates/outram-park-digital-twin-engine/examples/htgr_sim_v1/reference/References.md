## HTR-10 site and release parameters — `physics::atmospheric_dispersion`

Added 2026-09-24, replacing two placeholders whose doc comments stated the
published values were not available. They are now, from the same paper the
core inventory came from.

| Quantity | Value | Where used |
|---|---|---|
| Primary-circuit leakage | **~1 % per day** (`1.1574e-7 /s`) | `Htr10SiteInputs::LEAK_FRACTION_PER_S` |
| Stack height | **40 m** | `RELEASE_HEIGHT_M` |
| Reactor building height | **12 m** | `BUILDING_HEIGHT_M` — recorded, not used |
| Stack outlet velocity | **9 m/s** | `STACK_EXIT_VELOCITY_M_PER_S` — recorded, not used |
| Reference measurement distance | 7.5 km from site | not used; the receptor ring is 100–1000 m |

Source: Liu Yuanzhong and Cao Jianzhu, *Nuclear Engineering and Design*
**218** (2002) 81–90, pp. 4–5. Same access terms as the core inventory above:
restricted, no reuse licence, cited but not redistributed. Transcribed by the
maintainer through kovan's annotation tooling, 2026-09-24.

### What changed, and by how much

- Leak rate `1e-7` → `1.1574e-7 /s`, a **16 %** correction. The placeholder
  was a round number chosen to look like one, and it happened to be the right
  order of magnitude. That is luck, not vindication.
- Release height `30 m` → `40 m`. Larger than it sounds: ground-level
  concentration goes as `exp(-(z-H)^2 / 2 sigma_z^2)`, so this is a leading
  sensitivity.

### Two numbers recorded but deliberately not used

- **9 m/s outlet velocity** would drive a momentum plume rise, lifting the
  effective release height above the 40 m stack. This model has **no
  plume-rise term**, so the effective height is under-stated and the
  ground-level concentration correspondingly over-stated.
- **12 m building height** bears on wake entrainment. A 40 m stack against a
  12 m building is a ratio of 3.3, clear of the ~2.5 rule of thumb, so
  neglecting the wake is defensible — the constant is here so a reader can
  check that rather than take it on trust.

### Still missing, and it is the important one

A primary-circuit leak rate is **not** a release-to-environment fraction.
Between them sit building retention, filtration and the stack, none of which
this simulator models. The same paper's **Table 5** reports the airborne
activity that actually reaches the environment, and its **Table 7** the
resulting doses — ~~neither is digitised~~. **CORRECTED 2026-09-28** — Table 5
is now in the workspace: 22 nuclides, Bq released per year of normal
operation, unfiltered, at
`crates/changi/reference/htr10_normal_operation_annual_airborne_release.csv`,
exposed by `changi::activity::airborne_release`, provenance in
`crates/changi/docs/References.md`. **This simulator does not use it**: wiring
it in is a separate modelling decision. ~~Table 7 is still not digitised.~~
**CORRECTED 2026-09-28 (later the same day)** — Table 7 is now in the
workspace too: the published individual effective dose to adults, in mSv per
year, at 12 distances from 0.5 to 75 km along the worst azimuth. It is an
AIRDOS-EPA result for the normal-operation release, peaking at 1.4E-4 mSv/a
at 1.5 km. It is at
~~`crates/changi/reference/…`, exposed by `changi::activity::published_dose_by_distance`~~
**MOVED 2026-09-28** to
`crates/buangkok/reference/htr10_normal_operation_individual_dose_by_distance.csv`,
exposed by `buangkok::published::normal_operation_dose_by_distance`, provenance in

and it computes no dose. So the
concentrations here are still a circuit leak driven through a dispersion

| Quantity | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| K-40, ingestion, adult, committed effective dose equivalent per unit intake | **5.02e-9 Sv/Bq** | FGR-11 Table 2.2, row K-40 (f1 = 1.0), column "Effective", printed p. 156 (PDF p. 164) |
| Cs-137, ingestion, adult, committed effective dose equivalent per unit intake | **1.35e-8 Sv/Bq** | FGR-11 Table 2.2 cont'd, row Cs-137 (f1 = 1.0), column "Effective", printed p. 166 (PDF p. 174) |

**Source.** K. F. Eckerman, A. B. Wolbarst, A. C. B. Richardson, *Limiting
Values of Radionuclide Intake and Air Concentration and Dose Conversion Factors
for Inhalation, Submersion, and Ingestion*, Federal Guidance Report No. 11,
EPA-520/1-88-020, U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, 1988.
URL: `https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-05/documents/520-1-88-020.pdf`.
A U.S. government report, freely distributed by the EPA. Accessed 2026-09-28
(the epa.gov download and the maintainer's local copy are byte-identical,
SHA-256 `3d5479f4…8b74`). Cited, not redistributed — no PDF is committed.

**Processing.** The PDF's OCR text layer garbles exponents (e.g. the Cs-137
"Effective" entry extracts as `1.35 10..`), so both values were read off the
rendered table pages (PDF pp. 164 and 174 at 110 dpi), not from the text layer
and not from memory.

**Why FGR-11 and not FGR-13.** FGR-13 (*Cancer Risk Coefficients for
Environmental Exposure to Radionuclides*, EPA 402-R-99-001, 1999) was checked
as well. Its Chapter 2 tables are cancer **risk** coefficients per Bq — e.g.
Table 2.2a, "Mortality and morbidity risk coefficients for ingestion of water
and food", printed p. 84 (PDF p. 101), K-40 tap-water mortality 4.30e-10 per
Bq — not dose per unit intake, and no Sv/Bq table for K-40 or Cs-137 was found
in the report text. A risk coefficient is not a dose coefficient, so FGR-13
could not supply the anchor. FGR-11's coefficients are ICRP-30 "Reference
Man" (adult, occupational basis) values — adequate for an indicative anchor
and nothing more. No ICRP publication was used.

**Arithmetic.**

```text
floor ("≈ banana")            = 1.0e-7 Sv / 5.02e-9 Sv/Bq = 19.92 Bq  (K-40, ingested)
top   ("≈ 1 Sv-equivalent")   = 1.0 Sv    / 1.35e-8 Sv/Bq = 7.407e7 Bq (Cs-137, ingested)
span                          = log10(7.407e7 / 19.92)    = 6.570 decades
```

**Disagreement, reported rather than tuned away.** The maintainer's anchor was
stated as "~15 Bq of K-40, ~0.1 µSv". Through FGR-11 those are not the same
point: 15 Bq × 5.02e-9 = 7.5e-8 Sv, and 0.1 µSv / 5.02e-9 = 19.9 Bq. The floor
is derived from the cited coefficient, so it is 19.9 Bq (0.12 decade above
15 Bq on the log scale).

**Unit caveat.** The anchors are Bq of *intake*. The map paints an
instantaneous air concentration in Bq/m^3 (or Bq/m^3 per Ci). The anchors are
placed **numerically** on that scale; this is not a unit conversion, and no
breathing rate or dose is applied.

**What it means on screen (measured 2026-09-28, under the channel's default
3 m/s meteorology; the map has since moved to the fixed inter-monsoon puff
model below, and this Bq/m^3 figure was re-measured there -- see "Dose-rate
basis").** At a 1200 K kernel the
absolute release rate is 9.5 Bq/s (five tracked nuclides) and the peak
instantaneous concentration on the receptor ring is 1.7e-4 Bq/m^3 — about five
decades below the default floor. On the default scale a normal-operation plume
therefore paints nothing; the map says so, and the "minimum reading" slider
brings it into view.

Constants and tests: `FGR11_K40_INGESTION_SV_PER_BQ`,
`FGR11_CS137_INGESTION_SV_PER_BQ`, `banana_anchor`, `one_sievert_anchor`,
`tests::the_slider_defaults_are_the_cited_anchors` in `app/map_tab.rs`.

### GPU (petir shaders) and timing

The dose-rate map's per-pixel cost is the chi/Q field, which already runs on
petir's WGSL runner (`changi::puff::wgsl::field_auto` →
`petir::wgsl::gpu::GpuContext`), with changi's CPU pool as the no-adapter
fallback. The dose rate is that field times one f64 factor per pixel.
`physics::atmospheric_dispersion::tests::the_dose_rate_field_agrees_gpu_vs_cpu`,
512 × 512, 120 puffs, median of 5, this host (16 logical cores, one adapter,
run with `-j2`/`RAYON_NUM_THREADS=2` while other builds were running):

| Path | Time |
|---|---|
| GPU, petir WGSL (`field_gpu`) + factor | **1.5–2.8 ms** (three runs) |
| CPU, changi pool (`field_pooled`, the fallback) + factor | 174–181 ms |
| CPU, serial reference + factor | 282–345 ms |
| the factor pass alone | 0.06 ms |

Agreement: tolerance 1e-4 relative (changi's GPU budget for the f32 field)
+ one f32 ulp of the peak absolute (denormal flush in the tail). Measured:
worst relative 7.6e-7 within three decades of the peak, worst absolute
2.7e-7 of the peak. A separate shader pass for the factor would cost a
dispatch and read-back to save 0.06 ms, so none was added.
