## HTR-10 site and release parameters — `physics::atmospheric_dispersion`

Added 2026-09-24, replacing two placeholders whose doc comments stated the
published values were not available. They are now, from the same paper the
core inventory came from.

| Quantity | Value | Where used |
|---|---|---|
| Primary-circuit leakage | **~1 % per day** (`1.1574e-7 /s`) | `Htr10SiteInputs::LEAK_FRACTION_PER_S` |
| Stack height | **40 m** | `RELEASE_HEIGHT_M` |
| Reactor building height | **12 m** | `BUILDING_HEIGHT_M` — recorded, not used |
| Stack outlet velocity | **9 m/s** | `STACK_EXIT_VELOCITY_M_PER_S` — recorded, not used |
| Reference measurement distance | 7.5 km from site | not used; the receptor ring is 100–1000 m |

Source: Liu Yuanzhong and Cao Jianzhu, *Nuclear Engineering and Design*
**218** (2002) 81–90, pp. 4–5. Same access terms as the core inventory above:
restricted, no reuse licence, cited but not redistributed. Transcribed by the
maintainer through kovan's annotation tooling, 2026-09-24.

### What changed, and by how much

- Leak rate `1e-7` → `1.1574e-7 /s`, a **16 %** correction. The placeholder
  was a round number chosen to look like one, and it happened to be the right
  order of magnitude. That is luck, not vindication.
- Release height `30 m` → `40 m`. Larger than it sounds: ground-level
  concentration goes as `exp(-(z-H)^2 / 2 sigma_z^2)`, so this is a leading
  sensitivity.

### Two numbers recorded but deliberately not used

- **9 m/s outlet velocity** would drive a momentum plume rise, lifting the
  effective release height above the 40 m stack. This model has **no
  plume-rise term**, so the effective height is under-stated and the
  ground-level concentration correspondingly over-stated.
- **12 m building height** bears on wake entrainment. A 40 m stack against a
  12 m building is a ratio of 3.3, clear of the ~2.5 rule of thumb, so
  neglecting the wake is defensible — the constant is here so a reader can
  check that rather than take it on trust.

### Still missing, and it is the important one

A primary-circuit leak rate is **not** a release-to-environment fraction.
Between them sit building retention, filtration and the stack, none of which
this simulator models. The same paper's **Table 5** reports the airborne
activity that actually reaches the environment, and its **Table 7** the
resulting doses — neither is digitised. Until they are, the concentrations
here remain a circuit leak driven through a dispersion model, not a source
term, and `chi/Q` remains the only quotable output.
