## gh:#1060 — regime-map interfacial area, `u_int` friction, `H_int` mass transfer

### What changed

`tampines::multiphase_1d::interfacial` and `two_fluid`:

- **`A_int` from a regime map (Eq. 117).** Reused, not re-ported:
  GeN-Foam's `RegimeMap1D` (one-parameter map on `α_g`, linear blend) and
  `InterfacialArea` (`Spherical` `6α/d_b` for bubbly/slug, `Annular` `4α/D_h`
  for annular/mist, cutoff 0.9), from
  `crates/outram-foam-appbuilder-lib/src/genfoam/thermal_hydraulics/closures/interfacial/`.
  This adds the edge `tampines → outram-foam-appbuilder-lib` (no cycle).
  Windows: bubbly/slug `α_g ≤ 0.5`, annular/mist `α_g ≥ 0.75`, the TRACE V5
  void-only drag map (**not re-checked** against that manual here). The
  correlations take a diameter, so the area enters as the Sauter-equivalent
  `d_eff = 6α_g/A_int`; in the bubbly window `d_eff = d_b` exactly.
- **Interface velocity (Eq. 200).** `u_int = (α_lρ_lu_l + α_gρ_gu_g)/(α_lρ_l + α_gρ_g)`,
  the definition in the gh:#1060 brief (Eq. 200 itself not checked). The
  mass-transfer momentum is `Γ_k(u_int − u_k)` and the drag does work
  `F_k u_int`, which in this solver's static-enthalpy form is the source
  `F_k(u_int − u_k)`; the two phases' shares sum to `K_d(u_g − u_l)² ≥ 0`.
- **Total-enthalpy mass transfer (Eq. 208).** `H_int = h_sat + ½u_int²`
  becomes `Γ_k(h_k^sat + ½(u_int − u_k)²)` in static-enthalpy form. `Γ` is
  unchanged, because `H_g,int − H_l,int = h_fg` exactly.
- All three are on by default in `TwoFluid1d::new`; ablations
  `without_regime_map`, `without_interface_velocity`,
  `without_total_enthalpy_mass_transfer`.

Two edge cases of the reused map are handled and tested: its dropped point
at `α_g = 0.5` (weights sum to 0) is given to the bubbly window, and its
double-counted point at `0.75` (weight 2) is normalised away.

### Predictions (written before the first run)

1. Bubbly window: closures bit-identical to the fixed-diameter set.
2. All three ablated: the demonstrator blowdown is bit-identical to
   `43cb3eb5d`.
3. Demonstrator blowdown (8 cells, `α_g⁰ = 0.1`, 1 mm, 20 ms): the void stays
   below 0.5, so the regime map is inert; `u_int` moves void and outlet flow
   by < 1e-3; friction work moves `ΔT` by ≲ 1 %; `H_int` < 1e-5.
4. The low-void starts (`α_g⁰ ≤ 0.01`) still refuse at the same step: they
   stay in the bubbly window, where nothing in gh:#1060 changes the area.
   (The brief expected them to become runnable.)

### Results

|---|---|---|
| bubbly-window closures (`α_g` = 1e-4, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5) | bit-identical | held |
| `A_int` vs closed form (`α_g` = 0.625, 0.75, 0.8, 0.95) | rel ≤ 2.7e-16 | held |
| friction work sum vs `K_d Δu²` | 4.000000e4 W/m³ exact | held |

The `H_int` miss is not explained. The source is ~1 J/kg per kg transferred.
A candidate, not checked: the outer correctors stop on tolerances, so a small
perturbation can change the corrector count and the path of a 667-step march.

### Pin moves

## gh:#1062 — phasic wall heat in the drift-flux and two-fluid solvers

### What changed

The new `tampines::multiphase_1d::wall_heat::PhasicWallHeat` covers Eq. 201:

- `Hem(WallHeat)` reuses `tampines_steam_tables::hem::WallHeat`, the same
  type `HemPipe` takes: adiabatic (the default), a prescribed flux, or a
  wall temperature with a given `h`.
- `Relap5WallTemperature { T_w, geometry }` gets `h` per phase from the
  gh:#1063 dispatcher `relap5_wall_heat_transfer`. It uses each phase's
  density, viscosity, conductivity, `c_p`, a finite-difference `β` and
  velocity. It has single-phase convection only: no boiling curve and no
  CHF.
- The phasic split is the **linear wall-contact partition**: vapour `α_g`,
  liquid `1 − α_g`. It reuses GeN-Foam's `ContactPartition::Linear` and
  `Complementary`. gh:#1060 brought an interfacial-area regime map, not a
  wall-contact map, so it does not change this split.
- `DriftFlux1d::set_wall_heat` sums the two shares into its one energy
  equation. `TwoFluid1d::set_wall_heat` puts each share into its own phase.
  Both are explicit on the start-of-step state.

### Predictions (written before the first run)

1. A closed heated pipe at rest gains exactly the wall heat, apart from the
   reversible `α Δp` work. The residual is below 1e-9 of the heat input in
   both solvers.
2. A heated drift-flux channel (reservoir inlet, pressure outlet) reaches a
   steady state with a two-phase outlet. At steady state the outlet
   enthalpy equals `h_in + Q/ṁ` to 1e-4. (The energy equation has `∂p/∂t`
   and no `u ∂p/∂x`, so this is the exact discrete balance.)
3. Adiabatic default: every existing pin stays bit-identical.

### Results

| Check | Measured | Prediction |
|---|---|---|
| two-fluid closed pipe, `q'' = 1e5 W/m²`, 20 steps | worst residual 1.253e-10 of `Q Δt`; `p` +11.5 kPa | held |
| drift-flux closed pipe, same | worst residual 3.323e-11 | held |
| heated channel, `q'' = 2e6 W/m²` (first protocol) | balance held to 5.7e-16, but **outlet subcooled** (`ṁ` = 2.186 kg/s, twice my estimate) | **missed** (protocol) |
| heated channel, `q'' = 4e6 W/m²` | `ṁ` = 1.504650 kg/s, steady to 1.5e-7, outlet void 0.478, balance 1.26e-8 | held |
| RELAP5 wall in both solvers | both heat the fluid | held |
| existing pins (Edwards GS-1 RMSE 28.2 psia, Marviken 23, two-fluid) | unchanged | held |

The channel flux was doubled after the first run so that the case tests the
two-phase outlet it claims to test. The criterion was not changed.

### Not done

- No boiling curve, CHF, film boiling or rewetting.
- No implicit wall-temperature coupling.
- No two-fluid heated-channel run yet. It needs an inflow boundary, which
  is gh:#1076, below.

## gh:#1058 — variable flow area in the drift-flux and two-fluid solvers

### What changed

`Pipe1d::with_area_profile` takes the **reused**
`tampines_steam_tables::hem::AreaProfile` (`Uniform`, `Linear`,
`ConvergingDiverging`). It evaluates it through
`HemPipeGeometry::flow_area_at`, so the face areas are exactly `HemPipe`'s.
A cell's area is the mean of its two faces, as in `HemPipe`. The hydraulic
diameter scales as `D_h √(A/A_ref)`, which is exact for a circular section.

The mass and energy fluxes use the face areas, and the conservation updates
divide by the cell volumes. The two-fluid pressure (volume-residual)
Jacobian uses the face areas. The drift-flux pressure equation does too, and
its vapour transport scales each face flux by `A_face/A_cell`. Friction uses
the local `D_h`.

The momentum equations are in non-conservative velocity form. There the
quasi-1-D wall-pressure source `p ∂A/∂x` cancels exactly against
`A ∂p/∂x − ∂(pA)/∂x`, so no explicit area source appears. That is the
equivalent of `HemPipe`'s conservative `V dW/dt = −(FA)_e + (FA)_w + p ΔA`.
The drift-flux drift-stress term `∂φ/∂x` is **not** area-weighted. That is a
stated simplification, and it vanishes with zero drift.

### Predictions (written before the first run)

1. An explicit `Uniform(A)` profile is bit-identical to the constant section
   in both solvers. All existing pins are unchanged.
2. A closed vertical converging–diverging pipe keeps its mass to 1e-12.
3. A smooth subcooled-water nozzle (inlet/throat area 2, 0.2 m, 40 cells)
   gives face mass flows equal to 1e-6. Its inlet-to-throat drop matches
   Bernoulli to within 5 %, and the measured drop is the larger.

### Results

| Check | Measured | Prediction |
|---|---|---|
| `Uniform(A)` vs constant section, both solvers, 100 steps | bit-identical | held |
| existing pins (two-fluid bit pin, GS-1 28.2 psia, Marviken 23) | unchanged | held |
| closed nozzle, two-fluid inventory | drift −1.3e-16, flow 1.7e-2 m/s | held |
| closed nozzle, drift flux | end faces exactly 0 (inventory is EOS-based, so not checked) | **weaker check** |
| nozzle face mass flows | spread 8.3e-10 (`ṁ` = 41.11 kg/s) | held |
| nozzle `p_1 − p_t` vs Bernoulli | 50.24 vs 48.21 kPa, **+4.19 %** | held, close to the bound |

HemPipe was not run on the same nozzle. The analytic steady solution is the
reference.

## gh:#1076 — two-fluid pressure boundary, inflow state, time-dependent boundaries

### What changed

There are three new `TwoFluidBoundary` variants:

- **`PressureOutlet { pressure }`**: a pressure face. Each phase's face
  velocity comes from its own momentum equation over the half cell to the
  first centre. The 2×2 drag + virtual-mass block is assembled on the
  adjacent cell's state. Backflow is zero-gradient.
- **`PressureInlet { pressure, inflow }`**: the same face, and entering
  fluid carries `TwoFluidInflow`, evaluated at the boundary pressure.
- **`VelocityInlet { velocity, inflow }`**: a prescribed velocity on both
  phases with a described inflow.

`TwoFluidInflow` is a liquid temperature or enthalpy plus an inflow void.
The vapour enters saturated: `m_g = α ρ_g^sat`, `m_l = (1−α) ρ_l(p, h_l)`.

A pressure face's sensitivity enters the Newton (volume-residual) matrix
diagonal the same way an interior face's does.
`TwoFluidTimeDependentBoundary` covers the same three shapes, built from
the **reused** `tampines_steam_tables::hem::TimeTable`. It is evaluated at
`t^n` at the start of each step. `TwoFluidReport` now also reports
`inlet_mass_flow`, `inlet_energy_flow` and `outlet_energy_flow`, taken from
the fluxes the transport used.

### Predictions (written before the first run)

1. A velocity inlet delivers exactly the prescribed `ṁ` and `Ė` (1e-12).
2. A time-dependent boundary built from constant tables is bit-identical to
   the static boundary.
3. A pressure-driven heated two-phase channel (7.02 → 7.0 MPa, 0.5 m,
   `Q` = 6.28 kW) is steady to 1e-3 after 0.3 s, and `Ė_out − Ė_in` exceeds
   `Q` by less than 1 %.
4. Existing velocity boundaries: all pins are unchanged (the old paths are
   untouched).

### Results

| Check | Measured | Prediction |
|---|---|---|
| velocity inlet `ṁ`, `Ė` vs hand, 20 steps | 1.2e-16, 2.1e-16 | held |
| constant time-dependent vs static, 50 steps | bit-identical | held |
| heated channel after 0.3 s | `ṁ` spread 4.4e-4, but `Ė_out − Ė_in` = 5.13 kW vs 6.28 kW (**−18 %**) | **missed**: the channel was still storing energy (not steady) |
| heated channel after 1.5 s (revised protocol) | `ṁ` = 3.181829 kg/s, spread 4.1e-8; excess +1.64e-5; per-step budget residual ≥ 0, `D/Q` 3.7e-5 at the end | held |
| existing pins | unchanged | held |

While diagnosing the 0.3 s miss, I attributed a ~240 W per-step budget
residual to drag dissipation. The 1.5 s run shows that was wrong: the
residual is 0.23 W at steady state. The transient 240 W was **not**
diagnosed.

### Not done

- The pressure boundaries are static-pressure faces. No `½ρu²` stagnation
  correction is applied, unlike drift flux's `ReservoirInlet`.
- The convective acceleration is dropped at a pressure face.
- The inflow vapour is always saturated.
